# Methods

This is the methods section a paper that uses Aerolume can cite: what each solver solves,
how it is discretised, how its outputs are averaged, how uncertain they are, and what it
cannot do. It summarises the theory documents, which derive every choice and show the
measurements behind it:
[2D Navier–Stokes](theory_ns2d.md), [3D Navier–Stokes](theory_ns3d.md),
[airfoil polars](theory_airfoil.md), [finite wings](theory_wing.md) and
[sensitivity maps](theory_sensitivity.md). The evidence that the numbers are right is in the
[validation report](validation.md); its section 9 is regenerated by one command,
`python validation/run_all.py --all --write-docs`. To cite the software itself, see
`CITATION.cff` in the repository root.

Every result Aerolume saves carries a provenance record (section 10), so a methods section
can quote exactly what produced a number: the version, the solver settings and their hash,
the device and the precision.

## 1. The solvers at a glance

| solver | model | discretisation | validated against |
|---|---|---|---|
| `solvers.ns2d`, `solvers.ns3d` | incompressible Navier–Stokes, optional energy equation with Boussinesq buoyancy, actuator-disc fans, jets and vents | uniform staggered (MAC) grid, finite differences, SSP-RK3, exact projection, immersed bodies by Brinkman penalisation | Taylor–Green (exact), cylinder and sphere benchmarks, de Vahl Davis cavity, cubic cavity, heated cylinder and sphere, Rayleigh–Bénard onset, actuator-disc momentum theory |
| `solvers.airfoil` | 2D viscous–inviscid interaction | linear-vorticity panels + two-equation integral boundary layer with eᴺ transition, solved simultaneously by Newton's method | Ladson's NACA 0012 data, XFOIL 6.99 |
| `solvers.wing` | finite wings | vortex lattice (Trefftz-plane drag) and nonlinear lifting line, with viscous strip coupling to the airfoil polars | lifting-line and lifting-surface theory, Weber & Brebner and NACA wind-tunnel wings |
| `optimize` sensitivity maps | ∂(time-averaged force)/∂(surface displacement) | reverse-mode differentiation through the Navier–Stokes solver | central finite differences of the same discrete objective |

Everything is written in PyTorch (GPU or CPU, float32 or float64) except the airfoil and
wing solvers, which are NumPy/SciPy on the CPU.

## 2. Navier–Stokes solvers

### 2.1 Equations

Both solvers integrate, in non-dimensional form,

```
∂u/∂t + ∇·(u u) = −∇p + (1/Re) ∇²u + ∇·(2 ν_t S) − (χ/η)(u − u_s) + f
∇·u = 0
```

with lengths scaled by the reference length L (chord, diameter, vehicle length), velocities by
the reference speed U, time by L/U and pressure by ρU², so Re = UL/ν. χ ∈ [0, 1] is the solid
fraction of the immersed bodies, u_s their velocity (zero or a rigid rotation), η the
penalisation permeability, ν_t an optional subgrid viscosity and S the strain-rate tensor.
f collects the placed-physics forces: buoyancy (section 3) and fans and jets (section 4).
The flow enters along +x; angle of attack and yaw rotate the geometry, so the x force is drag.
Coefficients: C_d = F_x / (½ ρU² A_ref) with the reference area (2D: reference length per unit
span); pitching moments are positive nose-up (theory_ns2d.md §1, theory_ns3d.md §1).

### 2.2 Grid, boundary conditions, symmetry

A uniform staggered grid of cell size h (Harlow & Welch 1965): velocity components on the faces
normal to them, pressure and scalars at cell centres. The discrete divergence of the discrete
gradient is then exactly the compact Laplacian, which makes the projection exact (2.4). Inlet:
prescribed velocity (uniform, or a parabola for channel benchmarks). Outlet: convective
condition with the outflow corrected to the inflow at every stage. Side walls: slip, no-slip
or a moving road. A closed domain (`domain_mode = "closed"`) has walls on every side and no free
stream. In 3D a symmetry plane (`bc_y = "symmetry_ymin"`) is a free-slip wall, which for
incompressible flow is exactly a mirror plane; the solver reports full-body forces, and a
half-domain run reproduces the full one (validation.md §8).

### 2.3 Spatial discretisation

Advection is in conservative form ∇·(uu). Two schemes:

* `central`, second order and kinetic-energy conserving on a discretely divergence-free
  velocity (Morinishi et al. 1998): no numerical dissipation, used for verification;
* `upwind3` (default), the third-order upwind-biased reconstruction written as a
  fourth-order central flux plus a |V|-weighted fourth-difference dissipation, which stays
  stable at cell Reynolds numbers far above 2.

Diffusion is the 5-point (2D) / 7-point (3D) Laplacian. The Taylor–Green vortex converges at
order 1.97, 1.99, 2.00 with `central` in 2D and in each coordinate plane in 3D
(validation.md §1, §8).

### 2.4 Pressure

Every Runge–Kutta stage ends with a projection: solve L ψ = ∇_h·û, set u = û − ∇_h ψ. L is the
compact Laplacian with Neumann (or periodic) conditions, whose eigenvectors are known, so the
discrete Poisson problem is solved *directly* to round-off: in 2D with a packed DCT-II built on
one real FFT per direction (Makhoul 1980), in 3D with orthonormal DCT-II / discrete Hartley
matrices applied as matrix products, under IEEE float32 (TF32 is forced off; its 10-bit mantissa
would leave a divergence of about 10⁻³). Measured residual 4.1 × 10⁻¹⁴ (2D) and 4.9 × 10⁻¹⁴ (3D)
in float64; projected divergence max|∇·u| h/U of 1.8 × 10⁻⁷ (2D) and 4.0 × 10⁻⁷ (3D) in float32 on
the GPU. The pressure written to the output is the step-averaged one, (⅙ψ₁ + ⅔ψ₂ + ψ₃)/Δt, which
is the pressure of the discrete momentum balance (theory_ns2d.md §4).

### 2.5 Time integration

Three-stage strong-stability-preserving Runge–Kutta (Shu & Osher 1988) with a projection after
every stage, so every stage value is divergence-free. Δt = min(CFL h / Σ max|u_i|, C_v h²/(1/Re +
max ν_t)) with CFL 0.5 and C_v = 0.25 (2D) or 1/6 (3D), computed on the device every step (no
host synchronisation, which allows CUDA-graph capture). `adaptive_dt = "pid"` replaces the jump
to that limit by a PID-controlled change of Δt (bounded per step) that keeps the CFL target
smooth; the limit itself is never exceeded.

### 2.6 Immersed bodies

Bodies are Brinkman-penalised (Angot, Bruneau & Fabrie 1999). The solid fraction is a compact
C¹ half-sine step of the signed distance to the polygon (2D) or triangle mesh (3D), one cell
wide, evaluated directly at each face position. After each stage the penalisation is
integrated implicitly, u ← (u + kχu_s)/(1 + kχ) with k = b_s Δt/η, which is unconditionally
stable. The effective wall of a smoothed mask is not at d = 0; its offset was calibrated once
on plane Poiseuille flow between penalised slabs (an exact reference), giving a permeability
η = min(0.05 h/U, h²/(60 ν)) and an inward mask shift of 0.32 h that centres the remaining
offset on the true surface (theory_ns2d.md §6.3). The same calibration holds in 3D, where the
sphere is its independent validation.

**Forces** are the momentum the penalisation removes, summed with the Runge–Kutta weights:
F = −(h^d/Δt) Σ w_b (⅙Δ₁ + ⅔Δ₂ + Δ₃). For steady flows this is the discrete momentum balance
of the whole domain; a control-volume balance agrees with it to 0.1 % (2D cylinder) and
0.01–0.03 % (3D sphere), so it is the force of the discrete solution, not an estimate of it.
Surface pressure and friction are extrapolated from two probes outside the diffuse layer.

### 2.7 Subgrid models and limits of the flow solvers

Optional LES closures: Smagorinsky (Smagorinsky 1963; C_s = 0.17), WALE (Nicoud & Ducros 1999;
C_w = 0.5) and Vreman (2004; c = 0.07), with Δ = h and ν_t zero inside solids. There is **no wall
model and no transition model**: above Re ≈ 10⁴ the immersed walls are under-resolved and forces
are for comparing designs, not for quoting (validation.md §4, §8). In 2D the subgrid model is a
stabilising dissipation, not a turbulence model (2D turbulence has no vortex stretching).

### 2.8 Start-up and determinism

Shedding is triggered by a fixed weak Lamb–Oseen vortex (peak 5 % of U) placed on the bodies at
t = 0, not by random noise. The flow computation draws **no random numbers**, so the inputs
determine every field, force and heat rate. The one random element is display-only: 3D tracer
particles are released at uniformly random points of their emitter's footprint (section 5),
which changes where the dots are drawn but nothing that is computed. Floating-point reductions
on a GPU are not bitwise reproducible, and runs are not even guaranteed to repeat bitwise on one
machine: two runs of the same 2D Draft airfoil simulation (NACA 0012, α 4°, 640 × 384 cells, the
GPU shared with other work) gave C_d 0.096913 and 0.096940, a difference of 0.03 % or 0.03 of the
standard error of 0.00092, and C_l 0.0453 and 0.0442 (0.33 standard errors). Time averages
therefore differ within their statistical uncertainty (section 8), which is what the
reproduction check allows for (section 10).

## 3. Heat transfer

### 3.1 Energy equation and buoyancy

Temperature is carried as θ = (T − T_amb)/ΔT_ref and advanced inside the same SSP-RK3 stages as
the momentum:

```
∂θ/∂t + ∇·(u θ) = ∇·((1/Pe + ν_t/Pr_t) ∇θ) + q
f_buoyancy = Ri θ ĝ        (ĝ = −y in 2D, −z in 3D)
```

with Pe = Re Pr, a turbulent Prandtl number Pr_t = 0.85 when an LES model is on, the Richardson
number Ri = gβΔT_ref L/U² and q the volumetric sources. This is the Boussinesq approximation:
density changes only in the buoyancy term, valid for βΔT ≪ 1 (Gray & Giorgini 1976 quantify the
error for air and water). Buoyancy is switched off inside solids. Natural-convection cases are
scaled with the free-fall velocity U = √(gβΔT L), so Re = √(Ra/Pr), Pe = √(Ra Pr) and Ri = 1
(the validation cavities use this scaling).

### 3.2 Discretisation

Advection of θ uses a MUSCL reconstruction limited by van Leer's harmonic slope (van Leer 1974)
with the stage's discretely divergence-free face velocities. Each forward-Euler stage is total-
variation diminishing at the solver's CFL 0.5, and SSP-RK3 is a convex combination of such
stages, so θ gets no new extrema. Diffusion is in flux form with the wall conditions applied on
the boundary faces themselves, so the heat rates the solver reports are the fluxes the scheme
used: in a steady state the heat through one wall equals the heat through any section to
round-off (measured: hot-wall and cold-wall Nusselt numbers of the cavities agree to 10⁻⁷,
validation.md §9).

### 3.3 Thermal boundary conditions

* **Bodies.** "temperature": implicit penalisation of θ towards the body temperature with the
  momentum's mask, wall shift and stiffness rule, Pe in place of Re (η_T = min(0.05 h, h² Pe/60));
  the heat the penalisation adds is the body's heat-transfer rate, as its momentum impulse is
  its force. "heat_flux": the flux is released in a one-mask-width skin centred on the effective
  wall, normalised so the total is exactly flux × area. "adiabatic": conduction across the
  body is blocked.
* **Domain walls** (`WallThermal`): temperature (Dirichlet over half a cell), heat flux, or
  adiabatic; the tunnel inlet carries the ambient temperature and the outlet has zero gradient.
* **Heat sources**: a volumetric q over a smooth region, normalised so the released power is
  exactly the requested one on any grid.

### 3.4 Nusselt number and heat balance

Nu = Q L / (k ΔT_ref A), with Q the heat rate from the bodies into the fluid, k the fluid's
conductivity and A the heated area (perimeter per unit span in 2D); in solver units
Nu = Pe Q / (Δθ A), Δθ the body's temperature difference to the ambient in units of ΔT_ref.
A run's heat balance compares the heat added by bodies, walls, sources and jets
with the heat carried out by the flow and stored in the domain; its residual is reported with
every thermal result (Results page, report, CSV export), and when a solver does not report a
term the residual says which term is missing rather than assuming it zero
(`analysis.physics.heat_balance`).

## 4. Fans, jets and vents

**Fans** are actuator discs (2D: strips): a uniform force density (ΔP/t) n̂ over a disc of
thickness t (at least two cells), normalised separately on each face family so the total force is
exactly T n̂ on any grid, with optional swirl. Mode "thrust" sets ΔP = T/A; mode "velocity"
regulates T with a PI controller inside the step to reach a target disc-averaged axial
velocity. The fan report gives the delivered thrust, the volume flow, the mean induced velocity
and the ideal power T·u_disc. The reference for validation is actuator-disc momentum theory
(Rankine 1865, Froude 1889): C_T = 4a(1 + a) for a disc that accelerates the flow, 4a(1 − a)
for one that retards it, and, in a confined tunnel, the streamtube model of Garrett & Cummins
(2007) written with the signed pressure jump (`validation/run_all.py`, `confined_disc_theory`).

**Jets and vents** are mass sources and sinks: a jet adds the volume flow Q of its port with the
momentum Q U_j n̂, its temperature and its smoke; a vent removes fluid carrying the local
velocity, temperature and smoke. The projection solves L ψ = ∇_h·û − m, so the velocity field
has exactly the prescribed sources, and a tunnel's outlet carries the inflow plus Σm. In 2D the
port is a short body behind its open face whose velocity is also penalised towards the jet
speed, ramping from zero at the closed back to U_j at the face (a nozzle); in 3D the source and
its momentum act over the anti-aliased footprint alone. An incompressible fluid in a closed box
cannot gain volume, so the jets and vents of a closed domain should balance; when they do not,
the pressure solve removes the excess as a uniform sink over the domain (the compatibility
condition of the all-Neumann problem), and the 3D solver adds a note saying so to its result.

## 5. Flow visualisation: smoke and tracers

Smoke is up to four passive scalar channels (bounded MUSCL / van Leer advection, zero inside
solids, concentrations below 10⁻⁶ set to zero to avoid subnormal floats), released inside each
emitter's footprint at its rate, with pulses and start/stop times. Tracer particles are
massless: advected with the midpoint rule (RK2) and bilinear/trilinear interpolation from the
staggered faces (continuous release draws streaklines, pulsed release timelines), recycled
from a fixed pool. In 2D they leave fixed nozzle positions; in 3D area emitters release them at
uniformly random points of the footprint (an unseeded generator, so two runs place their dots
differently). Neither smoke nor particles affect the flow. A smoke rake and a pulsed emitter
across a symmetry plane give the same smoke, temperature and velocity fields in a half and a
full domain to 4 × 10⁻¹⁵ in float64 (validation.md §9).

## 6. Airfoil polars

A linear-vorticity panel method with a Kutta condition for the outer flow and a two-equation
integral boundary layer (momentum and kinetic-energy shape-parameter equations, Drela's laminar
and turbulent closures) on both surfaces and the wake, with the eᴺ envelope method for transition
and a lagged shear-stress equation in turbulent flow. All boundary-layer equations and the
edge-velocity relation are solved simultaneously by Newton's method (Drela & Giles 1987; Drela
1989), as in XFOIL; the implementation builds on mfoil (Fidkowski 2022). Drag from the wake
momentum thickness (Squire–Young), Kármán–Tsien correction for low Mach numbers. Limits:
maximum lift is over-predicted by 0.1–0.25 and stall is late (validation.md §6).

## 7. Finite wings

A vortex-lattice method with vortex rings on the mean surface, semi-infinite trailing legs along
the free stream (rebuilt at every attitude) and induced drag from the Trefftz plane (Katz &
Plotkin 2001), with Prandtl–Glauert compressibility; and a nonlinear lifting line (Phillips &
Snyder 2000) solved by Newton's method. Viscous runs couple each spanwise strip to 2D polars of
its section and Reynolds number; lift beyond a section's attached branch is clipped and flagged,
not predicted (theory_wing.md).

## 8. Statistics of time-averaged outputs

A force or Nusselt history is an autocorrelated signal that starts with a transient. Aerolume's
statistics (`analysis.stats`, applied identically to forces, Nusselt numbers, heat rates and fan
thrust) are:

* **Transient removal** by MSER-5 (White 1997), applied to the signal and to its squared
  deviation (shedding grows in amplitude while its mean barely moves).
* **Standard error of the mean** by non-overlapping batch means (Flegal & Jones 2010), with the
  batch size set from the integrated autocorrelation time with Sokal's automatic window (Sokal
  1997) and from the first zero of the autocorrelation, so that a batch spans whole shedding
  periods; at least 10 batches, otherwise the standard error is flagged unreliable.
* **Variable time steps** are resampled to a uniform grid with the same number of samples before
  any of this, so means are time-weighted and spectra are valid.
* **Convergence verdict** combining the relative standard error, the drift between the two halves
  of the averaging window and the amplitude trend; it drives early stopping.

A reported value "1.412 ± 0.004" is the mean and one standard error.

## 9. Discretisation uncertainty: grid-convergence studies

A single run says nothing about its grid error. `analysis.gci` implements the procedure of
Celik et al. (2008) (based on Roache 1994, 1998) for the same simulation on three or more
systematically refined grids, h₁ < h₂ < h₃:

* the observed order p from |ln|ε₃₂/ε₂₁| + q(p)| = p ln r₂₁, q(p) = ln((r₂₁ᵖ − s)/(r₃₂ᵖ − s)),
  solved by bracketing and bisection (the fixed-point iteration can find a spurious large-p root
  when r₃₂ ≫ r₂₁);
* Richardson extrapolation φ_ext = (r₂₁ᵖ φ₁ − φ₂)/(r₂₁ᵖ − 1);
* the fine-grid index GCI = F_s |ε₂₁/φ₁| / (r₂₁ᵖ − 1) with F_s = 1.25 for three grids and 3 for two
  (assumed order), reported relative and absolute (u_num);
* convergence type (monotone, oscillatory, divergent) with the unequal-ratio threshold, a
  warning below the recommended refinement ratio 1.3, and, with four or more grids, the order of
  every triplet and a least-squares fit φ = φ₀ + C hᵖ (Eça & Hoekstra 2014, without their
  weighting rules).

Two additions for the time-averaged results of an unsteady solver: when grid differences are not
larger than two combined standard errors the study is marked **noise-limited** and its order is
not used as if known (a Monte Carlo propagation with a fixed seed gives the spread the noise
alone causes); and the numerical band is combined in quadrature with the statistical band
(2 standard errors) for the total uncertainty, both parts always reported. The `asymptotic`
verdict compares the observed with the formal order (within 10 %, Oberkampf & Roy 2010 §8.7)
rather than Roache's ratio GCI₃₂/(r^p GCI₂₁), which equals |φ₁/φ₂| identically when p comes from
the same three grids at a constant ratio and therefore cannot fail.

The machinery is itself validated (validation.md §9, case `gci_cavity`): on the de Vahl Davis
cavity at Ra 10³ the 16²/32²/64² study gives p = 2.03 and a 64² band of ±0.00072 in Nu, and the
128² value lies inside it (0.69 of the half-width). The Richardson value of the three finest
grids lies within the spread of the benchmark's own values. A first version of that check, which
asked for the benchmark itself inside the 64² band, failed (1.9 half-widths): the benchmark's
printed values differ by 0.001 between quantities that are equal in the exact solution, more than
the band. A GCI bounds the discretisation error of the discrete problem, so it is tested against a
finer grid; comparison with a benchmark has to include the benchmark's own uncertainty.

The project finds grid studies by itself (`Project.grid_studies`: same design and settings apart
from the resolution settings, three or more resolutions), and the report adds a grid-convergence
section with the table and figure whenever one exists. A study whose domain size changes with the
quality preset is flagged: part of the change is then a blockage effect, not discretisation.

## 10. Reproducibility and provenance

Every saved result carries a provenance record (`project/provenance.py`, `Project.provenance`):
the Aerolume, Python, NumPy, PyTorch (with its CUDA build), VTK and OS versions, the GPU; the
simulation settings and the design as run, with SHA-256 hashes of each and of both together (the
same `config_hash` means the same computation was asked for, whatever the names); device,
precision, grid, wall time; and the random seed (none: the flow computation draws no random
numbers, section 2.8). When the run hands its plan to the record (`provenance.attach(result,
design, sim, plan=plan)`, called with the plan the run used), the record also holds the
planner's complete resolved settings, every solver-configuration field, and their hash
(`plan.solver_hash`); the record's `source` map says which parts it has and who captured them,
so a record without the plan says so rather than implying it. Hashes are of a canonical JSON
form (sorted keys, exact float representation, arrays by the SHA-256 of their bytes).

`Project.export_reproduction_script(sim_id)` writes a plain Python script that uses only the
public scripting API: it embeds the design and the settings *as run*, builds its own project,
runs the simulation and compares the new numbers with the recorded ones. Time-averaged CFD values
must agree within three combined standard errors (with a 0.1 % floor for steady flows); the
deterministic polar and wing values to 10⁻⁵ relative. The script exits with 0 when everything
agrees.

## 11. Validation

`validation/run_all.py` runs every validation case (verification, 2D and 3D flow, heat
transfer, fans and jets, airfoils, wings), compares each quantity with its cited reference and a
tolerance band fixed before the run, and writes section 9 of the validation report with its
figures; `--quick` runs a subset that touches every area in a few minutes. See
[validation/README.md](../validation/README.md) for how to run and extend it. Every run records a
digest of the code it measured, and `--check-docs` says whether the published section still
matches the code. In the run of 1 November 2026 on Aerolume 0.1.0 (25 cases, 30 minutes on
the development GPU with no other compute job running) 126 of 128 checks passed. The two failures stay in the report: the square
cylinder's drag at 32 cells per side (+5.9 % against a ± 5 % band; 48 cells per side passes at
+3.7 %) and the momentum-flux drift of a plane jet in a closed box (6.2 % against a 5 % band; the
jet core's momentum budget closes to 0.27 %, validation.md §10). Two bands that development
builds had widened after their results were known (square cylinder +8 %, jet drift 8 %) were
withdrawn before the first release.

## 12. Limitations

* **Turbulence**: no wall model and no transition model in the Navier–Stokes solvers; forces
  above Re ≈ 10⁴ are for comparing designs. 3D vehicle drag is not validated (a wall-unresolved
  Ahmed-type body reads about 2.2× its wind-tunnel drag, validation.md §8).
* **Diffuse walls**: the immersed boundary is one cell wide; sharp corners converge most slowly
  (square cylinder drag +3.7 % at 48 cells per side). Observed orders for forces between 1 and 2
  are expected, not the interior's 2.
* **Heat transfer**: Boussinesq only (small temperature differences); constant fluid properties;
  no radiation, no conjugate heat transfer inside solids, no phase change; the thermal wall uses
  the momentum calibration with Pe for Re, validated on the cavities, heated cylinder and sphere
  at moderate Pe only.
* **Fans** are actuator discs: no blade geometry, tip losses or blade-element performance; the
  swirl is prescribed, not computed.
* **Jets**: the spreading of a plane jet follows Bickley's similarity law, but conservation of
  its momentum flux is not validated: in the closed test box the jet's core loses 6.2 % of it
  over 16 port widths, which the core's momentum budget attributes to the box (validation.md §10);
  no free jet has been run.
* **Incompressible** CFD only; the polar and wing solvers have low-Mach corrections.
* **Airfoils**: maximum lift and stall are over-predicted; **wings**: maximum lift is not
  validated.
* **Not in this release**: a Lattice-Boltzmann solver and neural-operator surrogates (deferred).
