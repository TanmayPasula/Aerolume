Documentation / Theory

The Aerolume 2D Navier–Stokes solver — theory, numerics and verification

Module: aerolume.solvers.ns2d (config.py, core.py, poisson.py, geometry.py, solver.py, rollout.py; the placed physics of sections 15–23 in physics.py, scalar.py, particles.py, diagnostics.py). Verification and validation scripts: validation/ns2d_cases.py, validation/thermal2d_cases.py, validation/bench_ns2d.py; tests: tests/test_ns2d_core.py, tests/test_ns2d_solver.py, tests/test_ns2d_physics.py, tests/test_ns2d_validation.py and tests/test_ns2d_physics_validation.py (-m slow).

Part I (sections 1–14) is the validated flow solver. Part II (sections 15–23) adds the placed physics of DESIGN.md section 10 — smoke emitters and tracer particles, heat transfer with buoyancy, fans, jets and vents, painted obstacles, live editing, diagnostics — without changing a Part I number.

This document explains every numerical choice, why it was made, and what was measured to back it. All numbers quoted were produced on the development machine (RTX 4060 Ti 8 GB, i7-13700F, torch 2.14.1+cu130, Windows 11) by the scripts above.

1. Equations and scaling#

The solver integrates the incompressible Navier–Stokes equations with a Brinkman penalisation term that represents solid bodies, in non-dimensional form

∂u/∂t + ∇·(u u)=−∇p + (1/Re) ∇²u + ∇·(2 νt S) − (χ/η)(u − us)
∇·u=0

Lengths are scaled by the reference length L (chord, cylinder diameter, vehicle length), velocities by the free-stream speed U, time by L/U, pressure by ρU², so Re = UL/ν. χ ∈ [0, 1] is the solid fraction, us the velocity of the solid (zero, or rigid rotation for wheels), η the permeability of the penalisation, νt an optional subgrid eddy viscosity and S the strain-rate tensor.

Flow always enters from the left along +x; angle of attack is applied by rotating the geometry (DESIGN.md §3), so the x force is drag and the y force is lift.

Pressure coefficient: cp = 2 (p − pref) where pref is the mean pressure over the two inlet columns (in periodic mode: the domain mean). Force coefficients use the reference length force_ref (in units of L): Cd = Fx / (½ force_ref), Cl = Fy / (½ force_ref). The pitching moment is about moment_ref and positive nose-up. For flow from the left a nose-up rotation is clockwise in the x-right/y-up frame (the leading edge moves up), so Cm = −Mz / (½ force_ref) where Mz is the usual counter-clockwise moment. With this convention a positively cambered airfoil has a negative Cm about c/4 (verified, §11).

2. Grid and boundary conditions#

Uniform staggered (MAC, Harlow & Welch 1965) grid of nx × ny cells of size h covering [x0, x0 + nx h] × [y0, y0 + ny h]:

quantitylocationshape
ux-faces (x0 + i h, y0 + (j+½) h)(ny, nx+1)
vy-faces (x0 + (i+½) h, y0 + j h)(ny+1, nx)
p, χc, dye, ν_tcell centres(ny, nx)

The staggered arrangement couples pressure and velocity without checkerboard modes and makes the discrete divergence of the discrete gradient exactly the 5-point Laplacian, which is what makes the projection exact (§4).

Boundary conditions are imposed through two layers of ghost values built by pad_u / pad_v before every right-hand-side evaluation, so the interior stencils never branch:

  • Inlet (x = x0): u = 1 (or a parabola with discrete mean exactly 1 and peak 1.5 for channel benchmarks; Re is then based on the bulk velocity, as in Schäfer–Turek), v = 0 (odd reflection of v). The inlet column is re-imposed from ops.u_inlet at every stage, so the solution is differentiable with respect to the inflow.
  • Outlet (x = x0 + nx h): convective condition ∂u/∂t + Uc ∂u/∂x = 0 with Uc = 1, integrated by the same Runge–Kutta stages as the interior; v has zero gradient. After each stage the outlet column is shifted uniformly so that the outflow equals the inflow exactly (a device-side reduction, no host sync). Walls are impermeable, so the net boundary flux — and hence the sum of the Poisson right-hand side — is zero to round-off, which the all-Neumann pressure problem requires.
  • Top / bottom walls: v = 0 on the wall face; u ghosts mirror the first interior row so that the interpolated wall value is: slip → ∂u/∂y = 0; noslip → u = 0; moving (bottom only) → u = ground_speed (the rolling road of vehicle studies).
  • Periodic (verification mode): periodic in x and y, no inlet/outlet; the duplicate faces (u[:, nx] = u[:, 0], v[ny] = v[0]) are kept bit-identical.

3. Spatial discretisation#

Advection is written in conservative (divergence) form ∇·(u u); momentum fluxes are evaluated at cell centres (normal fluxes) and cell corners (cross fluxes) with the advecting velocity interpolated to the flux point, then differenced back to the faces.

  • central: 2nd-order average of the transported velocity. On a staggered grid with a discretely divergence-free velocity this divergence form conserves kinetic energy (Morinishi et al. 1998), so the scheme adds no numerical dissipation — the right choice for DNS and verification. Verified: inviscid periodic random flow keeps its energy to 1 part in 10⁴ over 50 steps (the residual is the O(Δt³) RK3 error).

  • upwind3 (default): the transported value at a face between b and c (neighbours a b | c d) is the 3rd-order upwind-biased reconstruction (−a + 5b + 2c)/6 for flow from the left (mirror for flow from the right). Written branch-free:

    φface=[7(b + c) − (a + d)]/12 + sign(V) [3(b − c) − (a − d)]/12
    flux=V · central4 + |V| · dissipation

    i.e. a 4th-order central flux plus a 4th-difference dissipation proportional to |V| (the same structure as Kawamura–Kuwahara, with a 3× smaller coefficient). It is robust at cell Reynolds numbers far above 2, which makes under-resolved high-Re runs stable without smearing resolved scales the way 1st-order upwind would. Being smooth (|V| is the only non-smooth element) it is also differentiable almost everywhere.

Diffusion: 2nd-order 5-point Laplacian for the molecular part; the subgrid part is the divergence of 2 νt S with νt at cell centres (diagonal stresses) and averaged to cell corners (shear stress), which conserves momentum and is dissipative.

4. Pressure: exact projection with a direct DCT Poisson solver#

Each Runge–Kutta stage ends with a projection: solve L ψ = ∇h·û and set u = û − ∇h ψ, where L = ∇h·∇h is the 5-point Laplacian with homogeneous Neumann conditions on all four sides. (Every boundary-normal velocity is prescribed, so the correction must not change it: zero normal gradient of ψ.)

With Neumann conditions on a cell-centred grid the eigenvectors of L are exactly the DCT-II basis functions, with eigenvalues λk = (2 cos(πk/n) − 2)/h² per direction. A 2D forward DCT, a division by λkx + λky and an inverse DCT therefore solve the discrete problem to round-off: no iterations, no tolerance, identical cost on every call — ideal for CUDA-graph capture and for autograd. The constant mode (null space of L) is set to zero; the right-hand side has zero sum by construction (§2).

Implementation (poisson.py), all in differentiable torch.fft:

  • A length-N DCT-II is computed with Makhoul's reordering (evens ascending, odds descending — one index_select), one real FFT of length N, and a quarter-wave twiddle Zk = e−iπk/2N Vk, k = 0..N/2. For real input that half spectrum holds every coefficient: Xk = 2 Re Zk, XN−k = −2 Im Zk.
  • The coefficients are never put back in natural order: the solver keeps the packed layout [Re Z0..Re ZN/2, Im Z1..Im Zm] and permutes the eigenvalues once at construction. The inverse rebuilds Z from the packed array (including the Nyquist identity Im ZN/2 = −Re ZN/2), untwiddles, runs irfft and undoes the reordering with one gather. Cost: two half-length complex FFTs per direction and a few elementwise passes — about 90 µs for a 1024×512 forward 2D transform inside a CUDA graph.
  • Periodic mode uses rfft2/irfft2 with the periodic eigenvalues.

Verified (test_poisson_is_exact, bench_ns2d.py verify): max|L ψ − f| / max|f| ≤ 4.1e-14 in float64 for even and odd sizes, Neumann and periodic (tests: < 1e-10 on random right-hand sides). Projected divergence of real solver states after full steps (cylinder; the fast tests also use a moving ground and a no-slip top wall): max|∇·u| h / U = 3.9e-16 (float64), 1.8e-7 (float32, GPU) — the quality bars are 1e-10 and 1e-5.

The pressure written to the state is the step-averaged pressure p = (⅙ ψ₁ + ⅔ ψ₂ + ψ₃) / Δt. Expanding the stages (§6.4) shows that the stage gradients reach un+1 with exactly these weights, so this p is the pressure of the discrete momentum balance: a control-volume balance evaluated with it reproduces the penalisation force to 0.1 % (§6.4). The last stage alone, ψ₃ / (b₃ Δt) (the first implementation), is not consistent: with stiff penalisation every stage also removes the velocity that the previous projection left inside the solid, so the stage potentials are not proportional to bs Δt. The difference is a harmonic, dipole-like field around each body; it is invisible in the far field but made the control-volume drag of the Re = 40 cylinder 6 % low and its stagnation cp 0.13 low. p is output only (fields, cp, surface data) and does not feed back into the dynamics.

5. Time integration and time step#

Three-stage strong-stability-preserving Runge–Kutta (Shu & Osher 1988) with a projection after every stage, written in the convex-combination form

ûs=lerp(us−1, u0, as) + bs Δt L(us−1),(a, b) = (0, 1), (¾, ¼), (⅓, ⅔)
ûs←implicit penalisation (§6),us = P(ûs)(projection, §4)

so every stage value is discretely divergence-free. SSP-RK3 is 3rd-order in time and its stability region contains a segment of the imaginary axis (|z| ≤ √3), which the purely central advection operator needs (forward Euler and SSP-RK2 do not have that property).

Time step — Δt = min(cfl · h / (max|u| + max|v|), 0.25 h² / (1/Re + max νt)) with cfl = 0.5 by default. The advective limit uses the sum of the component maxima, a safe bound for the 2D CFL number; the viscous limit 0.25 is below the SSP-RK3 limit for the 2D 5-point Laplacian (≈0.31). The reduction is computed on the device at every step and the step size stays a device tensor: there is no GPU→CPU synchronisation anywhere in a step, which is what allows CUDA-graph capture. (The specification suggested refreshing umax every K steps; doing it every step costs two reductions, ~1 % of a step, and is strictly safer.) A fixed dt can be configured instead.

6. Immersed boundaries: Brinkman volume penalisation#

6.1 Masks#

Bodies enter only through the solid fraction χ, evaluated from the signed distance d (negative inside) of each polygon directly at the u-faces and v-faces (and at centres for display and LES switch-off):

χ(d)=1d + δ ≤ −w
=½ (1 − sin(π (d + δ) / 2w))|d + δ| < w
=0d + δ ≥ w

with half-width w = mask_width · h (default 1 cell) and an inward shift δ = 0.32 h (§6.3). The profile is C¹ and has compact support: with stiff penalisation any non-zero tail of χ acts as a wall (a tanh profile would move the effective surface outwards by several cells). The signed distance is exact (distance to the nearest edge, sign from an even-odd crossing test), differentiable with respect to the vertices, and is taken from aerolume.geometry.sdf.signed_distance when that module is importable (private fallback otherwise). Masks are stored only on a window of cells covering the bodies plus a margin, so penalisation, force sums and SDF evaluations cost in proportion to the body, not the domain. The live solver sizes the window for ±25° of pitch about moment_ref, so angle changes from the GUI rebuild masks in place (captured CUDA graphs stay valid).

Rigid rotation (wheels, omega about center) enters as χ us with us = ω × (x − c); it is pre-multiplied into the window arrays so it costs nothing per step.

6.2 Implicit penalisation#

After the explicit part of each stage, the penalisation term is integrated implicitly over that stage's effective step bs Δt:

u←(u + k χ us) / (1 + k χ),k = bs Δt / η

which is unconditionally stable — η → 0 simply pins u to u_s wherever χ > 0 — and exactly solves the stage's backward-Euler penalisation equation. The projection follows, so the final field is divergence-free; the small velocity the projection re-introduces inside the solid is removed again at the next stage.

6.3 Choosing η and placing the effective wall#

A smoothed mask with stiff penalisation does not put the no-slip wall at d = 0: the wall sits wherever the penalisation rate χ/η overtakes the rate at which momentum is supplied into a cell. In a wall-bounded shear flow that supply is viscous (ν/h²), so the effective wall position depends on the stiffness S = h²/(ν η) alone. We measured it with an exact reference: body-force-driven plane Poiseuille flow between penalised slabs in a periodic box (flow rate → effective gap width → wall offset), averaging four sub-cell positions of the wall (scratch study, reproduced in this section's table):

1.00.00−0.16−0.24−0.30−0.33−0.36

(offset of the effective wall in cells, unshifted mask; negative = wall protrudes into the fluid). The offset saturates as S grows. Wider masks do not reduce the sub-cell scatter once S is large (≈0.45 cells peak-to-peak for every width) and make the offset more Re-dependent, so w = 1 cell. The permeability is then chosen to keep the stiffness on the flat part of the curve at every Reynolds number:

η=min(0.05 h / U, h² / (ν · 60))

The first bound keeps the inertial penetration U η below 5 % of a cell (high Re); the second keeps S ≥ 60 (low Re). Over S ∈ [60, ∞) the offset spans −0.24 … −0.38 cells, so the mask is shifted inwards by δ = 0.32 h, which centres that band on the true surface: measured offset +0.05 h at S = 60 and −0.11 h at S = 1000. The penetration length √(ν η) ≤ h/√60 ≈ 0.13 h and both bounds vanish as h → 0, so the method converges to the no-slip solution. η is a device tensor updated by set_inflow(re=…) without breaking captured CUDA graphs. This calibration uses only the Poiseuille problem; the cylinder benchmarks below are independent validation.

Remaining geometric uncertainty: for straight walls aligned with grid lines the effective wall position scatters by about ±0.2 h with the sub-cell position of the wall; on curved bodies this averages out around the perimeter.

6.4 Force evaluation#

The force on the bodies is the momentum the penalisation removes. Writing the stage increments of the penalisation as Δ₁, Δ₂, Δ₃ and expanding the Shu–Osher stages,

un+1=un + Δt (⅙ L₁ + ⅙ L₂ + ⅔ L₃) + ⅙ Δ₁ + ⅔ Δ₂ + Δ₃ (+ gradient terms)

so the force on body b over the step is

Fb=−(h² / Δt) Σfaces wb (⅙ Δ₁ + ⅔ Δ₂ + Δ₃)

(wb = body b's share of χ at a face; 1 on its own support when bodies do not overlap). For a steady flow the three weighted increments sum to exactly −Δt times the stage residual, i.e. this is the discrete momentum balance of the whole domain: the projection adds momentum only through the boundary pressure, so a control-volume balance evaluated consistently with the discretisation returns the same number (plus the rate of change of momentum inside the control volume). The moment uses the same increments with lever arms at the face positions. The method is cheap (two small einsums on the window per step), per-body, and differentiable.

Cross-check by a control-volume balance. For the steady cylinder at Re = 40 (32 cells/D) the drag from the momentum balance

Cdcv(r)=2 · ∮|x| = r [ −p nx − u (u·n) + ν (2 ∂u/∂x nx + (∂u/∂y + ∂v/∂x) ny) ] ds

with every quantity interpolated bilinearly from its own staggered position, is 1.6205, 1.6206, 1.6205, 1.6202, 1.6198, 1.6199 on circles 1.5, 2, 3, 4, 10 and 20 cells outside the surface, against 1.6190 from the impulse (0.1 %). The parts change a lot between those radii (pressure 1.14 → 1.99, viscous 0.49 → 0.12, momentum flux −0.01 → −0.49); only their sum is constant. The fast test test_pressure_closes_the_momentum_balance checks the same identity (to 0.3 %) on a coarse Re = 10 case. So the impulse is the force of the discrete solution — not an estimate of it — and its grid convergence (§11) is the force's grid convergence. Of the candidate evaluations named in the specification (penalisation impulse, control-volume balance, surface integration) the impulse is the cheapest, exact per body and differentiable, so it is the solver's force output; the other two agree with it once the output pressure is the step-averaged one (§4) and the surface sampling is second order (§6.5). A sharper interpolated direct-forcing IB was not needed: the calibrated penalisation meets every cylinder target at 32 cells/D (§11).

6.5 Surface distributions (cp, cf)#

FlowSolver.surface(n, mean=False) returns, per body, n points equally spaced in arc length with outward normals, cp and cf (instantaneous, or from the running means). The wall is diffuse — the penalised layer reaches (w − δ) h = 0.68 h into the fluid — so wall values are extrapolated from two probes along the outward normal at d₁ = 1.5 h and d₂ = 2.5 h, just outside the layer:

  • cp: linear extrapolation of the probe pressures to d = 0. This removes the centrifugal gradient ∂p/∂n ≈ ut²/R across the probe distance (up to 0.07 in cp at the cylinder shoulders at 32 cells/D).
  • cf = (2/Re) ∂ut/∂n at the wall, from the quadratic through the wall velocity at d = 0 (zero, or (ω × r)·t on a spinning wheel) and the tangential velocity at both probes, with u and v interpolated from their own faces (no extra averaging pass). This is second order. The first implementation's one-sided ut(d)/d at a single probe under-predicted the cylinder's friction drag by 15–20 % and put separation 5–7° too far aft. Only the molecular viscosity enters (WALE and Vreman ν_t vanish at walls anyway).

Measured on the cylinder (§11 table, columns Cd_surf, Cp_front, θsep): the integral ∮ (−cp nx + cf tx) ds reproduces the impulse drag to 0.0–0.1 % at Re 40, and to within 2.1 / 1.1 / 0.7 % (32 / 48 / 64 cells/D) at Re 100, where the boundary layers are thinner (fewer cells across them) and the deficit converges away with resolution. At Re 40 the stagnation cp is 1.12–1.15 (viscous flows have cp₀ > 1 at moderate Re) and separation is at 126.0–126.5° from the front, i.e. 53.5–54.0° from the rear (Dennis & Chang 1970: 53.8°; Coutanceau & Bouard 1977: 53.5°). The split between pressure and friction depends on the probe placement by ±1–2 % each (probes at 1/2 h or 2/3 h instead of 1.5/2.5 h moved Cd_f by +2.7 % / −2.0 % at Re 40, 32 cells/D); the total is more robust than either part.

Reference pressure. cp is relative to the mean pressure of the two inlet columns (DESIGN.md). In a confined domain the static pressure near the body is lower than at the inlet: in the 5 %-blockage cylinder domain of §11 the slip-wall pressure abeam the cylinder is cp = −0.09 (Re 40) and −0.05 (Re 100) relative to the inlet, with a local wall velocity of 1.04 and 1.02 U. Blockage and upstream length (the planner's domain choice) therefore shift cp near the body almost uniformly; forces are unaffected.

7. Subgrid models (2D LES)#

turbulence = "smagorinsky" | "wale" | "vreman" adds ∇·(2 νt S), with νt computed at cell centres from the velocity-gradient tensor (corner derivatives averaged to the centre) and the filter width Δ = h:

  • Smagorinsky (Lilly): νt = (Cs Δ)² √(2 S:S), Cs = 0.17.
  • WALE (Nicoud & Ducros 1999): νt = (Cw Δ)² (Sd:Sd)3/2 / ((S:S)5/2 + (Sd:Sd)5/4), Cw = 0.5.
  • Vreman (2004): νt = c √(Bβ / α:α), c = 0.07 (≈ 2.5 C_s²); in 2D Bβ = det(β).

The 2D field is treated as a 3D field without a third component, so the textbook formulas apply unchanged. WALE and Vreman vanish in pure shear (no eddy viscosity in laminar boundary layers); Smagorinsky does not (tests). ν_t is multiplied by 1 − χc, i.e. it is zero inside solids, and its maximum enters the viscous time-step limit.

Caveat: 2D turbulence is not 3D turbulence (inverse energy cascade, no vortex stretching). In Aerolume these models are a stabilising subgrid dissipation for under-resolved high-Re runs, not a turbulence model in the physical sense; results at Re ≫ 10⁴ are qualitative (see §12).

8. Passive dye#

A cell-centred smoke scalar is injected at the inlet as a comb of thin streaks (about 40 over the domain height) and advected once per step (forward Euler, final velocity) with a MUSCL flux limited by van Leer's harmonic mean slope, then clamped to [0, 1]. Concentrations below 10⁻⁶ are set to zero: the limiter otherwise lets far tails decay into subnormal floats, which are ~100× slower on x86 CPUs (measured: the CPU solver fell from 60 to under 10 steps/s as the dye tails spread). The dye is purely visual and does not affect the flow. It is set to zero inside solids (cells with χ > ½) after each update, so smoke never appears to pass through a body; placed emitters, further channels and tracer particles are in §17.

9. Start-up perturbation#

perturb = True adds a weak Lamb–Oseen vortex (peak swirl 5 % of U, core radius 0.75 × the body height) centred on the bodies to the impulsive-start initial field, then projects. The bodies start with a small circulation, the near wake is asymmetric from the first step, and periodic shedding locks in after a few tens of convective times instead of waiting for round-off (which takes 100+ time units and differs between CPU and GPU). It depends only on the body bounding box, so runs are deterministic and reproducible.

10. Performance: CUDA graphs, CPU path#

A step launches a few hundred small kernels (three stages of padding, fluxes, Poisson transforms, penalisation, projection, plus dye and bookkeeping). Under Windows WDDM a kernel launch costs ~27 µs, so eager execution is launch-bound (59 steps/s at 1024×512). FlowSolver keeps the state in static buffers and captures blocks of 10 steps (plus a 1-step graph for remainders) in torch.cuda.CUDAGraphs after a warm-up on a side stream (the warm-up steps are undone, so capturing never changes the trajectory). Nothing in a step synchronises with the host: dt is a device tensor, the history row is written into a device ring buffer with index_copy_, running means are accumulated in float64 device buffers. Live edits write into existing tensors (nu, η, masks), so graphs survive them; a different body count or a window that must grow triggers a lazy re-capture; any capture/replay error falls back to eager execution with a warning. Graph and eager paths run the same kernels and agree to float32 round-off (test: 1e-6 after 34 steps).

  • Placed physics (heat transfer, fans, ports, emitters): §22.

13. Differentiability#

step(state, geom, ops, dt) is pure torch with static control flow. Everything between the polygon vertices and the force coefficients is differentiable: SDF (exact distance to edges) → C¹ mask → penalisation → projection (linear, FFT-based) → force impulse. The upwind dissipation |V| and the van Leer limiter are the only non-smooth elements (measure-zero kinks). rollout(state, geom, ops, n, dt, checkpoint_every=k) advances n steps with torch.utils.checkpoint over chunks of k steps (memory O(n/k + k) fields), with a fixed dt (letting the CFL rule choose dt inside a differentiated rollout would make dt a non-smooth function of the design). step returns the coefficients [Cd, Cl, Cm] summed over the bodies (step_full: per body); multiply by ½ force_ref for the non-dimensional force. Gradients with respect to body vertices (checked against central finite differences in float64: relative error 2.3e-7), the inflow profile (ops.u_inlet) and the initial state are tested. build_geometry(..., window=…) keeps the window fixed so finite-difference comparisons evaluate the same discrete function.

15. Placed physics: design rules#

DESIGN.md §10 adds AeroJAX-style placed physics to the validated flow solver: smoke emitters and tracer particles, heat transfer with Boussinesq buoyancy, heated bodies and walls, volumetric heaters, fans (actuator strips), jets and vents, mouse-painted obstacles, live editing, diagnostics and a PID-controlled time step. Module physics.py (with scalar.py, particles.py, diagnostics.py); solver-unit specs in config.py (EmitterSpec, ThermalConfig + WallBC, HeatSourceSpec, FanSpec, PortSpec, ImmersedBody.thermal). Four rules keep the validated solver intact:

  • Off means absent. ops.phys is None unless a config asks for placed physics, and every branch in the step tests a static Python flag, so the default path runs the kernels it ran before. Measured with torch.profiler (cylinder, 1024×512, eager, median of three interleaved rounds): 524 kernels per step against 521 without the one addition to the default path — the default smoke is now cleared inside solids (§8; a few small window kernels) — and 3048 µs of GPU kernel time per step against 3034 µs (+0.5 %; an earlier session measured −0.3 %, i.e. the difference is within the noise of a GPU shared with other jobs). Re-measured with the final code, after the wall model of §18.4 joined the step behind its own static flag: still 524 kernels per step, and the clean-up's own kernels take 0.16–0.18 % of the step's GPU kernel time (medians of two runs of five interleaved rounds, all rounds within 0.14–0.23 %; python validation/bench_ns2d.py default-cost). That ratio is the robust figure: two whole-step kernel times measured separately differed by up to ±15 % between rounds on the shared GPU. Every test and validation number of the base solver is unchanged (the slow base-solver and placed-physics validations were re-run and pass).
  • Static structure, live values. Which features exist (heat transfer on, buoyancy on, the number of fans, smoke channels and emitter timing groups, whether ports or paint exist) is static and baked into a captured CUDA graph. Every value — footprints, positions, thrusts, wall temperatures, Ri, Pe — lives in a device tensor that FlowSolver rewrites in place, so dragging a heater or an emitter never re-captures a graph. A change of structure (a first fan, another smoke channel) rebuilds what it must and re-captures lazily on the next advance.
  • Exact totals. Every distributed source (heater power, fan thrust, port volume flow, a heat-flux body's heat) is normalised by the discrete integral of its footprint, so the released total is exactly the requested one on any grid and at any sub-cell position. Footprints use the bodies' C¹ step (smooth_step, half-width one cell for heaters and fans, half a cell for nozzles and ports), so they move continuously when an object moves by a fraction of a cell.
  • No host synchronisation inside a step: controllers, pulse clocks, particle release counters and diagnostics are device arithmetic, so all of it runs inside the CUDA graphs (graph and eager agree to 1e-5 with every feature on: test_cuda_graphs_match_eager_with_all_physics).

16. Heat transfer#

16.1 Energy equation and its discretisation#

With θ = (T − Tamb)/ΔTref the energy equation in solver units is

∂θ/∂t + ∇·(u θ)=∇·((1/Pe + νt/Prt) ∇θ) + q,Pe = Re Pr, Prt = 0.85

θ lives at cell centres, so the face velocities of the MAC grid are exactly the advecting velocities of a finite-volume flux. Advection is the MUSCL / van Leer flux of the smoke (§8) with boundary-aware ghosts (scalar.tvd_fluxes); diffusion is in flux form with the domain-wall conditions applied on the boundary faces themselves (scalar.diffusive_fluxes: Dirichlet over the half cell between wall and first centre, prescribed flux, or zero), so the heat rates the solver reports are the fluxes the scheme used. θ is integrated with the same SSP-RK3 stages as the momentum (each stage: explicit update, implicit penalisation toward hot bodies and jets, §16.2), and the Boussinesq force uses the stage temperature, so momentum and energy are coupled at third order in time.

Time step: a discrete maximum principle. A forward-Euler stage of the limited flux is a convex combination of neighbouring values when its coefficients sum to at most one: the MUSCL / van Leer flux contributes at most 2 c (face Courant numbers summed over both directions; the limited slope ratio is ≤ 2) and the explicit diffusion cd κ Δt / h², where cd is the largest diagonal coefficient of the discrete Laplacian with its wall conditions — 4 in the interior, 5 next to a Dirichlet wall (conductance 2 over the half cell), 6 in a corner between two Dirichlet walls (physics.diffusion_coefficient). The temperature step is therefore

Δt≤1 / ( 2 (max|u| + max|v|) / h + cd (κ + max νt / Prt) / h² )

and SSP-RK3, a convex combination of such stages, keeps θ within the bounds of its initial, boundary and body values. The momentum limits alone are not enough: each of them (advective 0.5 h / (|u| + |v|) and viscous 0.25 h² / κ) can be satisfied while their sum exceeds one. The first implementation did exactly that, and the heated cylinder at Re 40, 32 cells/D blew up (θ reached 4 × 10³² after 410 steps). With the bound, θ stays in [0, 1] (test_temperature_stays_bounded_in_a_tunnel); the cost is a smaller step where advection and diffusion are both significant (heated cylinder Re 40, 32 cells/D: 3.2 × 10⁻³ against the 6.9 × 10⁻³ the separate limits allowed; the Ra 10⁶ cavity on 128² cells needs 1.9 times as many steps per unit time, §21.1). A buoyant fluid parcel at rest accelerates at Ri |θ|, so the step is also kept below cfl √(h / (Ri max|θ|)).

16.2 Temperature bodies: implicit penalisation and the thermal wall#

A body held at θ_s ("temperature") is penalised exactly like the velocity:

θ←(θ + k χ θs) / (1 + k χ),k = bs Δt / ηθ

on the same window and masks as the momentum (cell-centred χ, the calibrated inward shift δ = 0.32 h of §6.3). The penalised conduction problem is the penalised Stokes problem of §6.3 with Pe in place of Re, so the thermal permeability uses the momentum rule with Pe: ηθ = min(0.05 h, h² Pe / 60). 16.3 Heat-flux and adiabatic bodies#

Every body that is not held at a temperature is insulated: the diffusivity on each face is multiplied by 1 − χ of those bodies (the Neumann penalisation of Kadoch et al. 2012), so no heat crosses an adiabatic body or a painted obstacle. A "heat_flux" body releases G = q″L/(k ΔTref) through its surface as a volumetric source in its skin, with the weight −dχ/dd (whose integral across the interface is exactly one) normalised per body so that the released heat is exactly G P / Pe (P the outline length inside the domain). Measured: in an adiabatic box the heat content grows at exactly that rate (1e-9, test_heat_flux_body_releases_its_heat). Its wall temperature is the skin-weighted mean of θ; against the exact conduction solution of a flux slab it reads high by 0.26 G h (16 and 32 cells per gap; 0.42 G h when the wall sits half a cell off a face) — a first-order bias of a quarter cell, 0.8 % of G D at 32 cells per D.

16.4 Domain walls, heat rates and Nusselt numbers#

WallBC sets each side to adiabatic, temperature or heat flux; in a tunnel the inlet is held at theta_inlet and the outlet has zero diffusive flux. The wall heat rates are the boundary-face fluxes of the scheme (2 κ (θw − θ1) per cell for a held side, κ G per unit length for a flux side), so a steady state balances to round-off: conduction between two held walls reproduces the exact linear profile and Nu = 1 to 1e-10 (test_conduction_between_walls_is_exactly_linear), a flux wall the exact slope (test_heat_flux_wall_profile_and_balance). This conservative wall flux is also what makes the cavity Nusselt numbers converge at second order (§21.1).

FlowSolver.heat_transfer() reports per body the heat rate (units ρ c_p U ΔT_ref L per span), the wall temperature and the mean Nusselt number based on the reference length, Nu = Pe Q / ((θw − θref) P) (DESIGN.md §10.2: Nu = Q L / (k ΔT A)); surface() adds the local Nusselt number from the same two-probe quadratic as cf (§6.5).

A temperature that depends only on the height is balanced exactly by the pressure: the force is then a discrete gradient that the projection removes, and a stably stratified box stays at rest to 1e-12 (test_stable_stratification_stays_at_rest).

Velocities convert to the benchmark's κ/L by multiplying by Pe, heat rates per unit wall length to Nusselt numbers by Pe.

16.6 Heaters and the energy balance#

A heater is a volumetric source of exact total power over a smooth rect or disc footprint (in an adiabatic box the heat content grows at exactly that power: 1e-10, test_heater_energy_budget_is_exact). FlowSolver.heat_balance() reports the terms of the energy budget with the keys aerolume.analysis.physics reads — heat in from bodies, walls, heaters, jets (the enthalpy their penalisation adds) and the inlet (advection + conduction), heat out through the outlet (advection) and vents, the storage rate, and the residual in − out − storage. The terms are the scheme's own fluxes at the end of the step: in a steady run with every heat path at once (a hot and a heat-flux body, a heater, a hot jet, a vent, a held and a cooled wall, inflow, outflow and buoyancy) the residual is 1.8 × 10⁻⁶ of the heat input (64 × 32 cells, re-measured with the final code; test_energy_balance_closes_in_a_tunnel checks 10⁻⁵ on a coarser grid); during transients it is O(Δt) because the Runge–Kutta stages see intermediate fields.

17. Emitters: smoke channels and tracer particles#

Smoke. Up to four channels (dye, dye1..dye3) share the bounded advection of §8 (one batched flux evaluation for all channels). Emitters — point, line (capsule), rake (nozzles), rect or disc footprints with an anti-aliased edge — relax their footprint toward their concentration rate while on; emitters of one channel with equal timing share one weight / target map, so many nozzles cost one fused update. The release clock (start, stop, pulse_period, pulse_duty) is device arithmetic on the simulated time, so pulsed puffs stay inside the CUDA graph. Smoke is zero inside solids (χ > ½) and painted obstacles, vents remove it with their fluid, and jets can release it on a channel. With emitters placed, the inlet comb on channel 0 is optional (dye_inlet).

Tracer particles (particles.py). Each nozzle owns a fixed ring of slots in one pool (particle_capacity, default 32 768), so a release recycles the nozzle's oldest particle, memory is fixed and every update — advection, kill, release — has static shapes. Particles advance by the midpoint rule (RK2) with the end-of-step velocity sampled bilinearly from the staggered faces where u and v live; they die when they leave the domain or enter a solid. A continuous emitter releases one particle per nozzle each time its accumulated rate · Δt / nozzles passes an integer — up to four per nozzle and step, each placed along the local velocity by the time since its threshold crossing — so the released number is rate · t to within one particle and a fast streakline stays evenly spaced. A pulsed emitter releases one batch per period from all its nozzles: a timeline. In uniform flow the paths are exact (x = xe + age to 1e-9, test_particles_follow_uniform_flow_exactly). particle_lines() returns streaklines (per nozzle, ordered by age) and timelines (per batch, ordered along the nozzles) as polylines. The planner keeps frame="wind" emitters fixed in the tunnel when it rotates the bodies for incidence; the solver itself has one frame.

18. Fans: actuator strips#

18.1 Momentum source#

A fan is an actuator disc — in 2D a strip of width across and thickness along its direction (default width/8, at least two cells) — that adds the body force f = T φ n̂ / ∫φ dA with φ the smooth box footprint evaluated on the u- and v-faces. The discrete integral of the force is exactly T n̂: in a periodic box (no walls, no outlet) the total momentum grows at exactly the thrust (1e-10, test_fan_adds_exactly_its_thrust_in_a_periodic_box). "thrust" sets T (per unit span), "pressure_jump" sets ΔP with T = ΔP · width.

18.2 Velocity mode: an in-graph PI controller#

"velocity" regulates ΔP so that the footprint-averaged speed along the axis, u_d, reaches the target. The plant's quasi-steady slope follows from momentum theory: with the free-stream component U along the axis, ΔP = T/D = 2 ud (ud − U), so dΔP/dud = 2 |2 ud − U| (U = 0 in a closed room). The PI gains make the loop gain one (kp = that slope) and the integral loop decay at ω = |target| / (2 D) (ki = 2 ω kp), with anti-windup by clamping the integral so ΔP stays within 50 max(target², 1). The controller state lives in the solver state (ctrl), so it runs inside the CUDA graph. Measured (tunnel, target 1.5, width 1, Re 100): 2.0 % short of the target at t = 3, 0.15 % at t = 7, 0.006 % at t = 12, no overshoot. The first gains were sized for the fan's own fluid mass only and ignored that most of an increment of thrust accelerates the far wake; that loop was four times too slow (2 % short of the target at t = 25).

18.3 Fan report#

fans_report() gives per fan the delivered thrust, the pressure jump, the volume flow (u_d × width), the disc and induced velocity (u_d minus the free stream along the axis) and the ideal power T u_d (instantaneous or time-averaged), plus the per-step history.

What it shows. The 2D closed fan room has no grid-converged answer. Its mean speed changes by up to 82 % between grids and by nearly a factor of two within one run, the rooms take 500–900 L/U to leave their start-up transient (the Draft plan runs 240), only the coarsest rooms reach a steady state — on the finer grids the 100-L/U means keep wandering by 8–92 % over the last 600 L/U, switching between flow states (one room-filling vortex with the fan in its fast outer ring, or several cells with the fan in a slow core, which is why the disc velocity ranges from 0 to 5.3 m/s) — and every room runs at 2.5–4.6 times the fan's own static velocity, with peaks of 6–16 m/s. A 1 N/m fan does not do that to a real room. The reason is the dimension, not the grid: the fan's power T u_d can only leave a 2D room through wall friction and the subgrid and upwind dissipation of the resolved eddies. 2D flow has no vortex stretching, so energy cascades to large scales (Kraichnan 1967) and gathers in a few room-sized vortices that dissipate little; in a 3D room the fan's jet breaks down into 3D turbulence, which dissipates the power in the jet and the room interior. The 2D numbers are therefore set by under-resolved wall friction and numerical dissipation, which is grid-dependent by construction.

The wall model, and why it is off. With no-slip ghost cells the wall stress is ν u₁ / y at y = h/2. On the Draft grid (h = 42 mm) the first face sits at y⁺ ≈ 170 for 2 m/s near the wall, where the turbulent friction of the law below is 9.6 times the resolved one (5.7 times at 1 m/s, 16 times at 4 m/s), and the resolved one scales with 1/h — the obvious suspect for the grid dependence. wall_model="werner_wengle" adds the Werner & Wengle (1991) law: inverting u⁺ = 8.3 (y⁺)^(1/7) for the friction velocity gives the explicit τ = (|u₁| (ν/y)^(1/7) / 8.3)^(7/4), which equals ν|u₁|/y at y⁺ = 8.3^(7/6) = 11.81 and exceeds it above; the solver adds the excess max(τ − ν|u₁|/y, 0) as a force τ/h on the face row next to each no-slip domain wall (u next to the floor and ceiling — relative to a moving road — and v next to the side walls of a closed box). So a resolved wall (first face in the viscous sublayer) is left exactly as it was. Against the log law u⁺ = ln(y⁺)/0.41 + 5.2 the power law's stress is within +4.5 / −1.7 % for 30 ≤ y⁺ ≤ 1000 (−8.7 % at 3000, −8.4 % at y⁺ = 12). The model is exact in its own terms (tests: the stress is the power-law u_τ² to round-off above the crossover and zero below, it only removes kinetic energy, it reaches only the wall faces, it is differentiable; tests/test_ns2d_wallmodel.py).

It does not rescue the 2D room. With the turbulent friction in place the rooms organise into one strong vortex sooner and the 12-cell room speeds up (4.18 m/s against 2.42): once the wall is the sink, the steady speed follows from T u_d ≈ τ(U) U × perimeter, which for τ ∝ U^(7/4) gives several m/s, and the 24-cell room still drifts by 39 %. Its three grids (2.30, 4.18, 3.35 m/s) are no closer to agreement than the three without it. Correct wall friction cannot replace the missing 3D interior dissipation. The model therefore stays opt-in (SolverConfig.wall_model, default "none"), so every validated case — and every planned run — is unchanged; it is the right tool for a 2D flow whose losses really are at the walls (a channel or a lid-driven cavity at high Re), not for a room.

What to tell the user. In a 2D closed room with fans, the delivered thrust is exact (§18.1) and the pattern (where the jet goes, where it recirculates) is meaningful, but speeds, the disc velocity and anything derived from them (volume flow, ideal power, convective heat transfer) are qualitative and grid-dependent, and Froude momentum theory does not apply (the fan recirculates its own wake). Use the 3D solver for rooms.

19. Ports (jets and vents) and painted obstacles#

19.1 Jets and vents#

A jet creates fluid and blows it out of its open face; a vent sucks fluid in and removes it. The port body — a region depth deep (default three cells) behind the face of a jet, beyond it for a vent — carries a uniform mass source of exactly speed × size per unit span, and the projection solves ∇·u = source instead of zero (L ψ = ∇·û − s). The velocity inside the body is penalised (with the momentum η) toward the field of that source: zero at the closed back, speed along the direction at the open face (a linear ramp, the continuity solution of a uniform source). Measured: the flux through the first face line past a jet's open face is speed × size to round-off (10⁻¹⁶ relative in float64; the test checks 10⁻⁹, test_jet_and_vent_in_a_closed_box_conserve_mass), the velocity there is 0.9987 speed, and the momentum flux two widths downstream is 1.023 × that of a top-hat (§21.5). A jet can carry a temperature (penalised in its body) and smoke; a vent removes smoke and heat with its fluid.

19.2 Mass balance and the Neumann compatibility#

A tunnel's outlet is corrected so that outflow = inflow + the ports' net volume flow (to 1e-10, test_jet_in_a_tunnel_balances_at_the_outlet). In a closed (or periodic) domain the all-Neumann pressure problem needs a zero-sum right-hand side; a balanced set of ports satisfies that exactly, and an unbalanced one is made compatible by spreading the excess as a uniform sink over the domain (the planner warns: physically the box would pressurise). The projection then stays exact (residual and divergence below 1e-10 in float64, test_unbalanced_port_in_a_closed_box_keeps_the_projection_exact).

The fluid that uniform sink removes takes its heat and smoke with it (review round 5 before the release): when the ports' net flow q is positive, the scalar sink src_neg (the vents' own m < 0) also carries −q/A on every cell, so θ and smoke lose q θ / A locally and the reported vent heat includes the leak. Before, the conservative scalar update kept them — a source +θ q/A everywhere — and a 64² closed adiabatic box fed by a θ = 1 jet (q = 0.1) with no vent climbed to mean θ 2.05 and max 2.77 by t = 21.4 while its heat balance still "closed" (heat out of the ports 0). Now max θ stays at 0.988, the mean reaches 0.910 (the well-mixed estimate 1 − exp(−q t / V) is 0.882; the jet's penalised footprint puts in a little more than q·1), heat out of the ports is 0.091 against 0.100 in, and the residual is 1.6 × 10⁻⁶. A net sink (only vents) needs nothing: the fluid the solve creates evenly arrives at θ = 0 and dilutes through the conservative flux.

19.3 Painted obstacles (explore mode)#

paint_disc(center, radius, erase=False) and paint_polygon(points) add or remove solid in a brush footprint; clear_paint() removes all. The paint layer is penalised to rest with the momentum η, insulates heat and clears smoke, and stays outside the differentiable body geometry (sensitivities never see it). Its solid fraction is evaluated on each grid's own points (centres, u-faces, v-faces) with the bodies' calibrated interface (one-cell C¹ step shifted 0.32 h inside the outline), and its force is the penalisation impulse weighted by the paint's share of the penalisation at each face — exactly the bodies' force rule. A painted disc is therefore the same obstacle as an ImmersedBody of that outline: drag 2.1338 against 2.1337 (+0.006 %), the same mask to 10⁻⁴ and the same residual velocity inside (test_paint_erase_and_clear, which allows 1 % and 0.02). (A first version averaged the face fractions from the centres and weighted the impulse by the solid fraction itself, which discounted the interface where most momentum is removed: half the body's drag.) Brush strokes rewrite tensors in place, so after the first stroke no graph is re-captured.

20. Live editing, diagnostics, solver trace and the PID time step#

20.1 Live edits#

set_inflow(re=…) (ν, η, and with heat transfer κ = 1/(Re Pr) and η_θ), set_bodies, set_emitters, set_sources(heat_sources=, fans=, ports=) and set_thermal(richardson=, prandtl=, walls=, theta_inlet=) apply without reallocating the grid or the flow: an edit that keeps the structure rewrites the existing tensors (tests check the Physics object is the same one and, on CUDA, that no graph is re-captured); a structural change rebuilds the placed physics, keeps u, v, p, θ and the smoke already in the flow, and re-captures lazily.

20.2 PID-controlled time step#

adaptive_dt="pid" replaces the jumpy CFL rule (it follows every change of max|u|, e.g. a vortex passing the location of the maximum) by a PID controller in log space that steers the step toward 0.9 × the stability limit: error e = log(0.9 Δtlim / Δtprev), update Δt = Δtprev exp(kI e + kP (e − e₁) + kD (e − 2e₁ + e₂)) with (k_P, k_I, k_D) = (0.10, 0.25, 0.02), growth at most 3 % per step and never above the instantaneous limit (including the temperature bound of §16.1). Measured (cylinder, 300 steps): largest growth 1.0019 per step, largest CFL number 0.459 (test_pid_time_step_is_smooth_and_stable holds 1.03 and 0.5).

21.3 Rayleigh–Bénard onset#

Linear stability between rigid isothermal plates predicts onset at Ra_c = 1707.76 with wave number k_c = 3.117 (Chandrasekhar 1961; Reid & Harris 1958), independent of Pr. The case (rb_config, run_rb_growth) is one convection roll: a box of width π/k_c with stress-free, adiabatic side walls (which hold exactly that mode), no-slip plates at θ = ±½, Pr 0.71, float64. 22. Limitations of the placed physics#

  • Boussinesq, constant properties. Density differences enter only the buoyancy term (valid for β ΔT ≪ 1, i.e. temperature differences of tens of kelvin in air); viscosity, conductivity and heat capacity do not depend on temperature. No radiation, no compressibility, no conjugate conduction inside solids: a body is isothermal, releases a prescribed flux, or is insulated.
  • Thermal wall. The penalised thermal wall sits within ±0.1 cell of the momentum wall (§16.2), but the wall temperature of a heat-flux body reads 0.26 G h high (§16.3, first order in h). Heat rates are second order; local wall gradients (nu_local) need ≳ 32 cells across the body to be within 1 % of the heat-rate Nusselt number.
  • Forced convection. Extrapolated to zero blockage, the cylinder's mean Nusselt number lies inside the spread of the published values (Re 40: within 0.13 % of Bharti et al. and of Lange et al.'s correlation; Re 20: −0.7 % and +1.7 %; §21.2). A finite domain raises it: +1.5 to +1.6 % at 5 % blockage, +0.2 % at 1.25 %. The published values themselves spread by 1–5 %, so quote Nusselt numbers with ± 2 % plus the blockage effect of the domain used.
  • Natural convection is validated for steady laminar cavities up to Ra 10⁶ (§21.1). Above that, flows become unsteady and the boundary layers thin as Ra^(−1/4): 256² cells at Ra 10⁶ already need 40 000 explicit steps, and turbulent natural convection (Ra ≳ 10⁸) is outside what a uniform explicit 2D grid resolves.
  • Fans are actuator strips: thrust and the momentum-theory induction are reproduced (§21.4); there are no blades, no swirl (2D), no fan curve beyond the "velocity" mode's regulated pressure jump. Induction is within 1.4–5.4 % of inviscid theory at Re 500.
  • Fans in closed 2D rooms are qualitative. The thrust is exact, but the room's speeds are not grid-converged (settled mean speed 2.4 → 3.4 m/s from 6 to 24 cells per fan width for a 1 N/m fan in a 4 × 3 m room, 2.3–4.2 m/s with the wall model, still drifting by up to 39 % after 1200 L/U; §18.4): with no vortex stretching the fan's power can only leave through under-resolved wall friction and numerical dissipation, so a 2D room spins up into room-sized vortices. Momentum theory does not apply in a closed room. Use 3D for rooms.
  • Ports impose a uniform source over the port body and a top-hat velocity at the open face (momentum flux 1.023 × the top-hat value two widths downstream). An unbalanced set of ports in a closed domain is made solvable by a uniform sink (the planner warns); the real box would pressurise. Jets in closed boxes lose momentum to the return flow (§21.5).
  • Emitters are visualisation: smoke is a passive scalar with the numerical diffusion of the TVD scheme (streaks narrower than ~2.5 cells smear); tracer particles are massless, follow the bilinearly interpolated velocity with RK2, and carry no diffusion or inertia — use them for streaklines and timelines, not for particle statistics.
  • Painted obstacles are outside the differentiable geometry by design: sensitivities and optimisation see only ImmersedBody outlines.
  • PID time step. adaptive_dt="pid" smooths the step for live viewing; every validation run above uses the CFL rule, and the PID step has been checked for stability (CFL ≤ 0.5), not for accuracy of transients.
