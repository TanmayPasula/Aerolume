Aerolume UI guidelines (v4)

Aerolume should look like engineering software that people have used for years — the lineage of ParaView, Qt Creator, JetBrains IDEs, Ansys Discovery, XFLR5 — not like a generated dashboard. It must still be easy: the workflow stays guided (Geometry → Setup → Simulate → Results → Compare → Report), but guidance comes from layout, defaults and precise labels, not from decoration and chatty copy.

v3 adopts semantic colour tokens whose contrast is tested in both themes, Atkinson Hyperlegible type, and dockable panels with saved workspaces. Everything below is binding; the code contract is src/aerolume/app/theme.py (tokens, hooks) and src/aerolume/app/widgets/docks.py (panels, workspaces).

v4 (0.1.0) brings the app closer to the website: it carries over the site's language, not its layout. The chrome takes the site's monochrome palette, ink on paper in light and near-black in dark (theme.SITE_LIGHT / SITE_DARK record the website/assets/site.css value each token came from, and tests/test_tokens.py checks them against the file), the site's 6 px controls and 8 px frames, its two buttons (filled ink, outline) and its ink focus ring with an offset. Every page, panel, workflow, shortcut and data colour stays as it was: plots, colour maps, status colours and the blue marks in the data views keep their meaning.

1. What to avoid (the "generated app" look)#

Each of these is a defect, not a style choice:

  • Navy backgrounds with neon teal/purple accents, glows, gradients, glass effects. (The dark theme's neutral near-black is the website's, with no glow or colour on it.)
  • Hero banners, animated art, marketing taglines or feature cards inside the app.
  • Cards everywhere; corner radii above 8 px (controls 6 px, framed blocks 8 px, as on the website); drop shadows.
  • Decorative pill badges. More than one status chip in view is a smell; status is text.
  • Dashboard KPI tiles with oversized digits. Results are tables with units and uncertainty.
  • Two-line navigation items with subtitles; numbered circles; coloured icon tiles; emoji.
  • Rainbow / jet colormaps.
  • Vague or cute helper text ("Watch the flow live", "Rank your variants", "What can I do?"). Explanations go in tooltips or the docs; labels are nouns, buttons are verbs.
  • Centered layouts and large empty margins in a desktop tool; inconsistent spacing.
  • A colour that is not a token. Pages set style hooks or read theme.tokens().

The Start page's study tiles are the one place with boxes side by side: they are a choice list with a schematic of what each study sets up (section 4), not feature cards. The Start page's flow motif beside the product name is the one drawing that carries no information: static, faint (line_strong) and a computed flow (section 4), the website's own motif, never animated and on no other page.

2. Colour: semantic tokens with tested contrast#

Themes. Light is the default: the website's ink on paper, neutral greys with no colour cast (the norm for CAE tools, and the site's look). The dark theme is the website's near-black, and System (Preferences ▸ Theme, View ▸ Theme) follows the operating system's light / dark setting and switches when it does. No navy, no saturated backgrounds.

Switching must be quick. A theme switch is one application style sheet, applied by theme.apply() in two linear passes (clear, then set): Qt's own "replace the sheet" path re-polishes every widget once per ancestor, which measured 2.1 s on a window with every page built (1,933 widgets) and 3.5 s with six results open (5,305 widgets); the two-pass swap measured 0.27 s and 0.60 s. tests/test_app_shell_perf.py keeps it under 0.8 s offscreen. Never walk widgets to re-polish them after a theme change; a widget that paints by hand re-reads its tokens in a slot on theme.changed. Every token is an opaque #rrggbb; theme.semantic_tokens(name) lists them, theme.tokens() adds the v1/v2 names as aliases (text = fg, panel = surface_2, border = line, accent_fill = primary, good / bad = success / danger, ...).

Contrast rule (WCAG 2.x, enforced by tests/test_tokens.py in both themes: 73 rules over 68 colour pairings, listed in theme.CONTRAST_RULES; a pairing may serve two rules, as text and as a checked border):

  • text (fg, fg2, muted) on every background it is drawn on, and blue text, resting (accent: "Running", data labels) and the step further (accent2): ≥ 4.5:1 (links are ink since v4 and hover to fg2, both text tokens);
  • status text on the surfaces and on its own *_soft background, and *_fg on a solid status fill: ≥ 4.5:1;
  • control outlines (control, also the scroll-bar handle), the focus indicator and the primary fill against the surfaces they sit on, the highlight marks, the field view's solids and every plot series: ≥ 3:1 (WCAG 1.4.11);
  • faint is for disabled controls only (exempt under WCAG 1.4.3) and stays ≥ 2.2:1;
  • line is decorative (1.4:1 light, 1.2:1 dark) and never the only edge of a control.

Measured on surface (v4): light fg 19.3, fg2 9.3, muted 6.7 (5.8 on bg, 5.5 on bg_subtle), accent 5.7, control 3.7 (3.2 on bg, 3.04 on bg_subtle, the closest pairing in the table, as the site's own --line-strong is); dark fg 15.4, fg2 8.1, muted 6.9, accent 7.9, control 4.1. Plot series at least 3.3:1 (light) and 6.0:1 (dark). v3 measured light fg 16.5, muted 6.2, control 3.9; dark fg 10.7, muted 6.2.

Rules of use. The chrome's interactive states are ink, as on the website: the primary button, checked boxes and radios, the current tab and step (a 2 px ink underline), links (ink, underlined), checked icons (theme.ACTIVE_ICON_TOKEN maps a call site's "accent" to fg), the focus ring and slider fills. The blue accent is kept for marks in the data views, where the selected or live item has to stand apart from ink outlines (the selected body in the geometry canvas, the seed rake, the drawn obstacle, "Running" and its progress bar). Status colours are for text, small glyphs and text on their soft background (QFrame[notice=...]), never a solid fill behind body text. highlight is for marks that mean "heat" or "live" — the radiator in a schematic, a running indicator — and never carries text.

3. Type#

Atkinson Hyperlegible Next for the interface and Atkinson Hyperlegible Mono for numbers in tables, code and the log (SIL OFL, bundled as TrueType in app/resources/fonts; provenance and checksums in its README). They were drawn for low-vision readers: 0 / O, 1 / l / I and 5 / S cannot be confused, which matters in a tool whose output is numbers. Qt cannot load the published WOFF2 files, so they are converted once (build_fonts.py). The Latin subsets have no Greek, superscripts above ³ or arrows: every font stack ends with Segoe UI, which Qt uses glyph by glyph (α, ν, ω, 10⁵, →). Preferences ▸ Interface font ▸ System uses Segoe UI throughout.

Nothing larger than 14 pt anywhere; Bold is not used at 9 pt (its hinting fills counters). Titles from 10 pt up are Medium, as the website sets its headings (v4; SemiBold before); 9 pt group titles stay SemiBold, where Medium is too close to Regular to rank. Atkinson SemiBold is about 7 % wider than Segoe UI Semibold on Windows and the Mono 14 % wider than Consolas: size columns from QFontMetrics, not constants. Numbers right-aligned in tables; proper symbols (α, Cd, Re = 8.2×10⁵, m/s, N/m).

Shape and spacing. 4 px grid. Controls 24 px high; table rows 22–24 px; radius 6 px on inputs and buttons (theme.RADIUS, the website's controls), 8 px on framed blocks — Start tiles, cards, the tour's step box (RADIUS_LG, the site's frames) — 3 px on marks inside a control (check-box indicators, scroll-bar handles), 0 on panels (panels are separated by 1 px lines). Buttons are the site's two: secondary is an outline in control that turns ink on hover, primary is filled ink. Table headers have no fill of their own and a line_strong rule under them. No shadows, no gradients. Focus: a 1 px focus (ink) border on inputs and buttons; the primary button and a checked box, whose fill is the focus ink, get the website's offset ring instead — a 1 px fg ring with one pixel of the panel showing inside it (the fill is clipped to the content box; the geometry does not change, so nothing moves when focus arrives); custom-painted widgets use theme.paint_focus_ring, and the Start page's study tiles paint it only for keyboard focus (Tab, Shift+Tab, F6 or a key press: the site's :focus-visible), not for the window's initial focus or a click. Checked state: a checked tool button or segment is a primary_soft fill inside a 1 px fg border — the fill alone is 1.1–1.5:1 against the panels, the border ≥ 3:1 (WCAG 1.4.11, the "checked indicator" rules). A focused checked tool button shows the panel in its 2 px padding between ring and fill; a focused segment gets a 2 px ring (border and padding always add up, so nothing moves). Rich-text links are re-laid out on a theme switch (theme.apply), since Qt bakes the link colour in when the text is set. Widgets with no border of their own get a stronger mark: a group header (text on the panel) paints a 2 px focus ring around its arrow and title, a slider's handle a 2 px focus ring, and borderless item views (flat, pane: the Project tree, the Start page lists) keep a transparent 1 px border that focus colours, so the content never moves. Every focusable widget of the Setup and Start pages changes pixels on focus in both themes (tests/test_app_shell_release.py); v3.3 found the "Heat transfer" header, the angle slider, the Project tree and both Start lists unchanged.

An independent audit of the pairs the style sheet draws beyond the rule list (v3.1) found three that the list had missed, all fixed and now in it: hovered links in primary_hover (4.37:1 on the dark surface; then accent2, fg2 since links turned ink in v4), scroll-bar handles in line_strong (1.7–1.9:1; now control), and the primary button's 1 px focus ring (above; since v4 the offset ring).

Icons. 16 px monochrome line icons with a 1.25 px stroke in fg2; ink (fg) for the active state (v4; it was the blue accent). One coherent set, drawn as SVG (resources/icons, tests enforce it). Every icon engine applies the active-state mapping: the theme's bundled set, the hand-drawn glyphs of the parameter forms (Points, Dimensions), the live-edit tools and the 3D camera presets (tests/test_tokens.py checks all four in both themes).

Motion. None except progress indication. (The Start page's flow motif is a still picture, drawn once per size and theme.)

Accessibility (WCAG 2.1 AA, desktop reading; checked by an automated pass over the shell in both themes - every visible widget of the Start page, each workspace on four pages, the tour and Preferences):

  • Every input has a name a screen reader can read. A label merely placed beside a field does not give it one in Qt (measured: QAccessible...text(Name) is empty), so form rows go through PropertyGroup.add_row, common.tie_label(label, field) or a QFormLayout row with a string label. A composite editor's buddy is its first focusable part; its other parts (a unit selector) are named "<label>: <tooltip>". A page the window hosts but does not build (Preferences ▸ Assistant) is passed through common.tie_form_labels(page), which does the same for every addRow(QLabel, field).
  • Everything a pointer can do has a keyboard path: a toolbar button through its menu action, a panel through View ▸ Panels (Alt+1 … Alt+7), the account menu through its shortcut, the tour through Esc / arrows / Enter.
  • Focus order follows the screen, page first (WCAG 2.4.3). Tab goes step bar → page → the panels (left rail, right rail, bottom rail, each in rail order) and wraps round; MainWindow.focusNextPrevChild sorts Qt's chain by region and keeps Qt's order inside each. Qt's chain is creation order, and the panels are built before most of a page, so v3.4 counted 29 stops of panel chrome, Messages filters and Diagnostics between the step bar and the Setup form's first field. The panel headers' collapse, ⋯ and × buttons are not Tab stops at all: F6 / Shift+F6 cycle the regions as in Office and VS Code (step bar, page, then each panel on screen), land on a panel's header (2 px focus ring), where Enter or Space collapses or expands it, Shift+F10 or the Menu key opens its ⋯ menu, Delete hides it and Tab goes on into its content. F6 back to the page returns to the field that last had focus there. The header takes focus only from F6, never from a click, so dragging a panel never takes focus from the editor beside it.
  • Text contrast comes from the tokens (above); the automated pass measures the pixels of every fully visible label and button text, cropped from a grab of the whole window (a transparent label grabbed alone renders on black).
  • Scroll areas are not Tab stops of their own when their content takes focus, or when it fits: Tab reaches the fields and the area scrolls each into view. common. unfocus_scroll_areas runs after every page switch; a read-only area that overflows keeps its stop for keyboard scrolling and is named after its first heading. v3.3 counted four nameless stops on Setup (the rail holder and three scroll areas).
  • Target size (WCAG 2.2 SC 2.5.8): every button is at least 24 × 24 px (theme. MIN_TARGET; IconButton never goes below it, whatever size is asked: the panel headers asked for 22, the Messages bar for 20), or meets the spacing exception: a 24 px circle on its centre meets no other target's. The test measures it on Setup and Geometry. Since v3.4 the style sheet makes the common controls 24 px tall outright rather than leaning on the exception: a check box's whole row (min-height: 24px; it was 16 px, the indicator's height), the segments of SegmentedControl (borderless, so the 18 px content height every bordered button uses left them at 22 px) and the main toolbar's buttons (they were 22 px; the window chrome grew by 2 px). The account button in the menu bar is still 35 × 22 px — the menu bar sizes its corner widget to its own row — and passes by the spacing exception, with no other target within 24 px.

v3.1 pass (both themes, 1600 × 1000; Start, the four workspaces on Geometry / Results / Simulate / Setup, the tour, the three Preferences tabs): no text under its WCAG ratio except behind the tour's scrim, which dims the window on purpose while the step box (at full contrast) is read. Unnamed controls found and fixed in the shell: the nine fields of Preferences ▸ Assistant. Still unnamed, in pages the shell does not own: the Geometry page's parts table and one combo box, the Assistant's goal box, and the Script panel's editor, output, script list and "More" button.

Every side panel — Project, Messages, Assistant, Script, Properties, Flow visualisation, Diagnostics — is a dock with the same header: collapse chevron, title (9 pt SemiBold on bg_subtle), optional extras (the Messages counters), an optional on/off switch, the ⋯ menu and ×. What a user can do with any panel:

  • collapse it to its header (chevron, double-click on the header, or ⋯ ▸ Collapse);
  • reorder it in its rail (⋯ ▸ Move up / Move down, or drag the header);
  • move it to the left, right or bottom rail (⋯ menu);
  • hide it (× or ⋯ ▸ Hide panel) and bring it back from View ▸ Panels (Alt+1 … Alt+7);
  • run a rail in solo mode (⋯ ▸ Solo mode): opening one panel collapses the others there;
  • resize a rail by its edge; a double-click on the edge restores the workspace's widths.

Header switches toggle a panel's effect while keeping every value (an A/B switch): Diagnostics ▸ record on/off; flow-visualisation layers. A setting that is part of a form stays a check box. The switch is a 28 × 16 track: off = control outline, on = primary fill with the knob at the right (the state never relies on colour alone). A switch belongs to the content it was enabled for (DockPanel.enable_switch(..., content=), default: the content on screen), so a page's switch shows only while that page fills the panel and never stands in for the window's own.

Workspaces (toolbar right end, View ▸ Workspace): Default (Project left, the log folded at the bottom), Analysis (Assistant and the open log beside the results), Live (Flow visualisation on the right of a live run, Diagnostics under it), Scripting (the Script panel takes 40 % of the window height, the Assistant on the right). Each keeps its own arrangement across restarts; Reset puts the preset back. Panels never float. A preset that changes gets a higher rev, and an arrangement saved from an older revision is replaced by the new preset once (Live is rev 2: Diagnostics' list-beside-plots content needs 592 px, which in the right rail left the 3D Simulate page 798 of 1600 px and, capped at 1280 px, hid its plots behind a sideways scroll bar). Scripting's bottom rail is a share of the window height, not pixels (rev 2): stored in pixels and re-learnt from the rail's geometry, it shrank to whatever a small window had left (173 px after a 1280 × 800 session) and the editor showed three lines at 1600 × 1000. A share-sized rail is learnt only from a drag of its edge (accepted between 15 and 60 %), and the Script console keeps the same share across window resizes. Rev 3 lowered the share from 54 to 40 % ("shares": {"bottom": 0.40}, at least 300 px): at 54 % the Setup page above kept about 300 px, a 180 px 3D preview and truncated Estimate and Planner lists (v3.4). Measured over a Heated room's Setup page: the Script panel 400 of 1000 px with 20.6 editor lines and the page 448 px at 1600 × 1000; 314 of 800 px with 15.2 lines and the page 334 px at 1280 × 800.

A new study never opens in a tool workspace. Scripting is for a task, not a study's starting layout: a study created there opened with an empty "Untitled" script over half the window. DockManager.study_workspace() names the workspace a new study opens in — the current one, or when that is Scripting, the last other workspace the user had (kept in the settings file as workspaces/last_study; Default when there was none). The Messages log says so, and View ▸ Workspace ▸ Scripting brings the Script panel back with its text.

The page area reports the current page's minimum. A QStackedWidget takes its minimum from every page it holds, so after a visit to Results (432 px minimum height) the bottom rail could never have the height Setup (334) or Simulate (274) give up. main_window._PageStack reports only the page on screen; a switch to a taller page hands the height back from the rails. The Geometry page still needs 431 px, so at 1280 × 800 the Script panel over it shows about 9 lines.

Narrow windows. Below 1180 px the right rail folds into the left one as tabs and comes back at 1220 px, where it was. Below 1400 px each side rail is capped at 28 % of the window width (a panel's own minimum, the Assistant's 320 px, still wins): at 1280 px a content minimum used to widen the Live workspace's right rail to 400 px and leave the 3D view about 350 px wide. At every width a side rail is also held to what leaves the page its own minimum width beside the other rail (DockManager.page_guard_caps): without it a 592 px panel moved to the right rail at 1400–1520 px squeezed the 3D Simulate page to 598–640 px against its 711 px minimum and cut it off. Pages must still work at 1280 × 800 with one rail open.

Sharing the bottom rail. Opening Script or Diagnostics folds Messages to its title row (its counters and newest line stay visible); two open panels plus the log left the page 160-250 px of height at 800 px.

On the Start page the project panels step aside, but the Assistant and Script panels (which need no project) stay when wanted: a lit toolbar toggle always means a panel on screen.

Panels with nothing to show (Properties, Flow visualisation and Diagnostics on a page that provides none) are hidden and their View entry is disabled with the reason as its tooltip — never an empty panel. Pages fill them through dock_panels() (docks.py).

  • Project tree (left): designs with their simulations nested; status glyph per simulation (not run, queued, running with %, done, failed, stopped); context menu (new simulation, run, duplicate, rename, delete). Names elide at the end; the tooltip has the whole name. Selection drives AppState.
  • Step bar: flat tabs with a 2 px ink underline on the active step and a small check on completed ones. No numbers in circles, no subtitles. The breadcrumb at its right end names the design and the simulation, once when both carry the same name (the physics templates name a case's simulation after its design; "Heated cylinder · d 10 mm · ΔT 10 K › Heated cylinder · d 10 mm · ΔT 10 K" took 400 px).
  • Properties (in a page or the Properties panel): a property grid — label column (fg2), editor right, groups with a thin header line and a collapse arrow; units inside or right of the editor.
  • Messages: what the planner decided and why, warnings, errors, the run log. Folded to its header by default (counts and the newest line); it opens by itself for errors only.
  • Status bar carries device, run progress and units — no device chip in the toolbar.

4. Pages#

  • Start (no project open): the product line (mark, 14 pt name, one sentence) with the website's flow motif in the room to its right (pages.start.FlowMotif: short streaks along the streamlines of inviscid flow past a cambered Joukowski airfoil at 4°, the same flow the website draws, in line_strong, fading in from the left; one cached pixmap per size and theme, no timer, no focus, no accessible name; the mark inverts on the dark theme); New study as one row of tiles — Airfoil (2D), Vehicle (2D), 3D body from a mesh, Wing (3D), Heated room (3D, heat transfer), Electronics enclosure (2D, heat transfer: the room takes tens of minutes on a Draft GPU run, the enclosure 204 s, so a first heat study can finish while the user watches). When the width cannot hold every tile at its title's width (beside the Assistant at 1280 px) the row wraps into balanced rows (three and three), never wider than the window. Every tile has one height, the tallest description's at the tiles' current width; each tile reports its own width change and the page re-fits after the grid has settled (fitting on the page's resize used the old, wider widths, and on the first show at 1600 × 1000 the descriptions lost their third line). Measuring and painting share one text box (StudyTile._text_width): they had differed by the 1 px border inset, enough to wrap a line one word earlier when painted than when measured. Each tile has a line schematic of what the study sets up (free stream or closed box, the body, the heated part in highlight), its dimension and one sentence of what it starts with; the tiles are buttons (Tab, Enter, Space) with the sentence as their accessible description. Below: Recent projects (name, type, folder, modified) and Examples (3D first) beside an Open column (Open project…, Open example, More study types…) and Learn (Welcome tour, Scripting guide, Experiment assistant, Driving Aerolume from Claude, Keyboard shortcuts). Footer: Documentation, Validation, Licence and the version. Each help link once. At 1280 × 800 the tiles, Recent projects and the first examples (the heat-transfer ones among them) are above the fold; the rest scrolls (131 px with five recent projects and seven examples). Seven examples ship with finished results: the 3D sedan, the tapered wing, the NACA 2412 section and the sedan-fastback pair, plus three heat-transfer ones (the de Vahl Davis cavity at Ra 10⁵, the heated cylinder at Re 40 and a 2D room with a fan, a heater, a supply jet and a vent), listed after the 3D ones with a thermometer, so every Results tab (Heat transfer, Fans) has a finished study to show.
  • Geometry: CAD-like viewport — field_bg, light grid, outline in fg with a light grey fill, dimension lines in fg2, hatched road. Inspector = property grid.
  • Setup: property grid groups (Analysis, Flow, Attitude, Ground, Mesh & time, Physics: emitters, heat, fans, ports; Solver (collapsed)). Right column: the Estimate table and the planner's messages as plain lines with a small glyph.
  • Simulate: the viewport takes the space. Small viewport toolbar (field, colormap, overlays, fit). Vertical colour bar at the right edge of the viewport. Right panel "Run": thin progress bar, phase, t, steps/s, ETA; "Monitors" plot; "Explore" (live) collapsed.
  • Results: one header line of facts plus a single warning line when needed, then tabs.
  • Compare: table first (metrics × variants, deltas and %), plots below.
  • Report: form + list of generated files.

4b. Live editing (Explore, AeroJAX-style)#

  • Drawing obstacles, moving emitters, fans and heaters happens in the viewport with the tools of a small tool strip (brush, polygon, eraser, move; brush size; undo / redo / clear). A tool is a checkable icon button with a tooltip naming it and its key.
  • What is drawn shows as the solids do (neutral grey, 1 px outline); a placed item shows as its glyph with handles while selected. Heat sources and hot bodies use highlight.
  • Changes apply to the running flow without a restart; the Run panel says so in one line ("Obstacle added at t = 12.4") rather than with a toast.
  • Diagnostics (divergence, change per step, CFL, dt, residual) live in the Diagnostics panel, logarithmic where they span decades, exportable as CSV; the solver trace opens as its own tab of that panel.

5. Plots and fields#

  • pyqtgraph styled like publication plots: surface background, 1 px axes in fg2, grid alpha ≈ 0.15, small boxed legend, axis labels with units. Averaging windows are a light grey band, not a saturated fill.

  • Colormaps: speed viridis; Cp coolwarm (centred on 0); vorticity RdBu_r (centred); smoke greyscale on a dark-grey field; ν_t magma; temperature θ inferno from the ambient (heat is the one field where warm colours mean warm). Never jet/rainbow.

  • Solids are drawn in field_solid with a 1 px field_solid_line outline.

  • 3D colour maps (viz3d.colors.FIELD_INFO_3D; the same lookup tables as 2D):

    fieldmaprange
    speed |V|, velocity uviridisspeed from 0
    velocity v, w; pressure p; Cpcoolwarmcentred on 0
    skin friction Cf (and the wall-shear magnitude τ)viridisfrom 0
    vorticity |ω| (a magnitude in 3D)magmafrom 0
    Q-criterion; sensitivity dJ/dnRdBu_rcentred on 0
    eddy viscosity ν_tmagmafrom 0
    solid fractiongreyscale0–1

    Ranges are robust percentiles; diverging fields are symmetric about 0.

5b. 3D viewer#

The 3D view (app/widgets/view3d.py) follows the same restraint as the 2D canvases; it should read like ParaView or a CAD viewport, not a game.

  • Background is field_bg, flat — no gradient, no skybox, no vignette. A ground plane with a light grid only where there is a road.
  • Bodies without a field are the neutral field_solid grey, smooth-shaded under a white light kit with low specular, crease edges at 45°; triangle edges only on request.
  • Fields on surfaces and slices use the maps above. Slices are unlit, so a colour matches its colour bar exactly. Streamlines are thin tubes coloured by speed; smoke is greyscale. Emitters show as small highlight glyphs where they release.
  • Colour bar vertical at the right edge, title with units, end triangles when values exceed the range. It never covers the model.
  • Camera: perspective 25° (parallel on request); presets Iso, Front, Side, Top, Back, Bottom; turntable orbit; left drag orbits, right / middle or Shift+left pans, the wheel zooms toward the cursor, double-click or F fits.
  • Symmetry-plane results are always shown whole (mirrored).
  • Empty and failed states are plain text in the viewport ("3D view unavailable" with the reason). Never a blank grey rectangle.

5c. The assistant#

  • A suggestion arrives as a card listing every change, old value → new value; nothing is applied without a click; every value is re-validated against the editor's limits; one suggestion is one undo step.
  • Project data reaches a model only with consent, and the panel says which provider runs (Claude, or a local Ollama model). No API key is ever shown back.
  • Claude Desktop and Claude Code can drive Aerolume without a key in the app through the aerolume-mcp stdio server (Help ▸ Driving Aerolume from Claude).

5d. Start-up, tour, errors#

  • Splash (while PyTorch and VTK load): a plain 520 × 300 panel — mark, name, one sentence, the step being loaded ("Loading PyTorch…") and a thin bar. No art.
  • Welcome tour (first start; Help ▸ Welcome tour): steps over the real window, each dimming everything but the widget it is about; Back, Next, Skip tour; Esc skips. A tour explains only what is on screen, so it has two parts: on the Start page three steps (New study, Examples, Learn); with a project open five (step bar, Project panel, Run and Stop, workspaces, Script and Assistant). Finishing the Start part runs the project part the first time a project opens; skipping either part ends the tour for good. A step whose widget is hidden (a panel the user closed) says how to show it instead of pointing at nothing. A step about two controls outlines both (Run and Stop; Assistant and Script), and a box for a toolbar control sits below the whole toolbar, never over the neighbouring buttons.
  • Unexpected errors open one dialog per burst: the one-line error, a warn notice that the app keeps running and where the report was saved, the redacted report (home folder as ~) with Copy report and Open log folder.
  • One instance: opening a project while Aerolume runs opens it in the running window.
  • The desktop: creating or opening a project writes its project.aerolume marker (the file the installer's association opens) and, in an installed build, adds it to the taskbar jump list; folders inside the program (the bundled examples) are never written.
  • Check for updates (Help) is off until a release feed is set (Preferences ▸ Release feed; there is no published feed yet). It is one GET of that address on a worker thread and sends nothing; the answer says the version found, or why the feed could not be read.

6. Copy#

Sentence case. Labels are nouns ("Angle of attack", "Ground clearance"); buttons are verbs ("Run", "Import…", "Export CSV…", "Copy report"). Status is factual ("Not run", "Running 43 %", "Done in 3 min 24 s", "Failed — see Messages"). An action keeps its name through the flow (the Run button, F5 and "Run again" all say Run). Errors say what happened and what to do, never apologise. No exclamation marks, no emoji, no second-person cheerleading. Explanations live in tooltips and the user guide; a number the program filled in is shown, not hidden.
