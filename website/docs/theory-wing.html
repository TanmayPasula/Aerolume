Finite-wing analysis: theory and validity

Aerolume's Wing analysis predicts the lift, drag, pitching moment, lateral loads and span loading of a wing from its planform and the 2D polars of its airfoils, in the spirit of XFLR5: seconds per polar instead of the minutes-to-hours of 3D CFD. Two methods are available:

Vortex lattice (VLM, default)Nonlinear lifting line (LLT)
Modelring vortices on the cambered mean surfacehorseshoes on the quarter-chord line
Planformsany: low AR, sweep, dihedral, kinks, sideslipstraight or mildly swept (≤ ~20°), AR ≳ 4
Liftlinear (potential flow), no stallnonlinear: section lift curves from the viscous polars up to cl_max
DragTrefftz-plane induced + strip profile dragTrefftz-plane induced + strip profile drag
Use fordesign-point loads, any shape, momentslift curve towards stall, stall onset

Validation against theory, lifting-surface results and wind-tunnel data is in Finite-wing validation. Code: src/aerolume/solvers/wing/ (geometry.py, vortex.py, vlm.py, llt.py, polars.py, solver.py).

1. Geometry#

A wing is a WingSpec: a list of WingSection stations (root first) joined by straight lines. Leading edge (xle, y, zle), chord and twist vary linearly in y between two stations, so the leading and trailing edges are straight and the planform is exactly what the user typed. symmetric=True mirrors the stations about y = 0 (the sections describe the right half, y ≥ 0, design contracts 7.2); a first station at y > 0 leaves a gap at the centre (two separate lifting surfaces).

  • Twist rotates a section nose-up about the spanwise axis through its leading edge. Dihedral is the slope of zle in y; camber offsets are applied along the local surface normal (tilted by the dihedral), so a dihedral wing's sections stay perpendicular to its surface.
  • Airfoils are NACA 4/5-digit codes (generated) or design:<id> (a 2D airfoil design of the project, resolved by the caller). Only the mean line enters the vortex methods (thin-wing theory). It is taken as the average of the upper and lower surfaces at equal x/c in the chord-line frame of the polar solver (prepare_coords), so camber, section incidence and polar angles share one reference line. Between two stations with different airfoils the mean line and, in viscous runs, the section polars are blended linearly with the span position.
  • Spanwise panels (n_span per half, spacing "cosine" or "uniform"): the distribution is defined over the whole half span and split at every section station, so kinks fall on panel edges and each segment gets panels in proportion to its share (at least one). "cosine" clusters panels at the tip only (y = (b/2) sin θ, which makes the full wing the classical full-cosine distribution y = (b/2) cos θ) or, when the root is a free edge too, at both ends.
  • Control points sit at the midpoint of the mapped coordinate θ, not of y (Multhopp's "semicircle" rule). Lift falls like √(distance) at a tip; collocating at the θ-midpoint makes the discrete solution of an elliptic wing exactly elliptic (e = 0.997–0.999 with any panel count, against e = 1.025 at 24 panels per half and first-order convergence with y-midpoints).
  • Chordwise panels (n_chord) are uniform, with the vortex at 1/4 and the control point at 3/4 of each panel (exact 2D lift and moment for flat and parabolic mean lines). The control-point normal uses the local mean-line slope at the 3/4 point. The panel's corner points would give the secant slope, which is the slope at the panel midpoint: an O(Δx) error that put NACA 2412's zero-lift angle at −1.87° instead of −2.12° with 8 chordwise panels (now −2.117°).

Planform metrics (planform_metrics(wing)) are exact for the piecewise-linear planform: area is the planform area projected on the x–y plane, span the tip-to-tip distance in y, aspect_ratio = span²/area, mac = ∫c² dy / ∫c dy with its leading-edge x and spanwise station, taper, the root-to-tip quarter-chord sweep_c4_deg and mean_chord = area/span.

2. Frames, references and signs#

The wing is solved in its own frame (x aft, y spanwise, z up). Following design contracts 7.2, the angle of attack α rotates the wing about +y through the moment reference by +α (nose up) and the sideslip β (FlowConditions' yaw_deg convention) rotates it about +z by −β first. Equivalently the free stream seen by the wing is

v̂∞ = (cos α cos β, cos α sin β, sin α)

(freestream_direction); positive β gives the flow a +y component, so the −y half is windward. Forces and moments are rotated back to wind axes: CD along the free stream, CY along +y, CL along +z; C_roll, Cm and Cn are moments about +x, +y and +z through moment_ref (right-hand rule; Cm > 0 is nose up). Coefficients use q·ref_area; Cm also ref_chord, C_roll and Cn ref_span. Defaults: planform area, MAC and span, and the moment reference at the quarter chord of the MAC (x = x_le,MAC + MAC/4, y = z = 0). A wing analysed from a project uses the design's ref_area, length_ref, span_ref and moment_ref3.

Strip outputs (WingPoint arrays, both halves, ordered in +y): cl_local = 2Γ/(V∞ c) (the section lift coefficient based on the free stream; for the lattice Γ is the strip's total bound circulation), ccl = c·cl/c̄ with c̄ = area/span (its span average equals CL), alpha_i the induced angle [deg] and cd_local the strip profile drag.

3. Vortex-lattice method#

Each panel of the mean surface carries a vortex ring: its leading segment on the panel's quarter-chord line, its trailing segment on the next panel's quarter-chord line (a quarter panel behind the trailing edge for the last row) (Katz & Plotkin 2001, §12.3). The last row's side segments continue as semi-infinite trailing legs along the free stream (rebuilt at every α and β, so the wake leaves the trailing edge with the flow); their strength is the trailing-edge ring's, which is the Kutta condition. Shared segments are evaluated once with their net circulation.

The Biot–Savart kernels (vortex.py) are the singularity-free forms used by AVL and Katz & Plotkin: a finite segment a→b induces (r₁×r₂)(|r₁|+|r₂|)/(4π|r₁||r₂|(|r₁||r₂|+r₁·r₂)) and a semi-infinite one (d×r₁)/(4π|r₁|(|r₁|−d·r₁)); points closer than 10⁻⁶ of the segment length to its line (its own midpoint included) get zero. Flow tangency at every control point gives a dense linear system A Γ = −V∞·n; the bound-vortex part of A is built once per wing, only the wake legs change with the flow direction. 8 × 48 panels solve in ~25 ms per angle on this machine.

Forces. Kutta–Joukowski F = ρ Γ (V × dl) on every bound segment (spanwise and chordwise), with V the free stream plus the velocity induced by the whole system at the segment midpoint ("near field"): lift, side force and all moments come from these. Induced drag comes from the Trefftz plane: the trailing legs, projected on the plane normal to the free stream, are 2D point vortices; Di = −(ρ/2) Σ Γs (v·n)s Δss over the trace segments, with the normal-wash evaluated at the same semicircle fractions as the lattice. The near-field drag of a lattice needs the leading-edge suction a lattice cannot resolve and converges poorly; the far-field value is accurate on coarse lattices (an elliptic wing gives e = 0.997–0.999). The strip induced angle is half the Trefftz-plane downwash angle (the lifting-line relation).

Compressibility (Prandtl–Glauert / Göthert). Influence coefficients are evaluated on the geometry stretched by 1/β in x (β = √(1−M²)), velocities are mapped back as (u/β, v, w), and the tangency condition uses the physical normals. Γ, hence the Kutta–Joukowski lift and the Trefftz drag, carry over unchanged (tested: CL(M) equals CL/β of the incompressible wing stretched by 1/β). Valid for subcritical flow; above M 0.6 the result carries a warning, M ≥ 0.9 is refused.

4. Nonlinear lifting line (Phillips & Snyder 2000)#

Horseshoe vortices with bound segments on the quarter-chord line between the spanwise nodes and trailing legs along the free stream. At each control point the 3D vortex lifting law is equated to the section lift:

R_i = 2 |V_i × dl_i| Γ_i − |V_i|² cl_i(α_i) dA_i = 0
V_i = v̂∞ + Σ_j v_ij Γ_j,    α_i = atan2(V_i·u_n, V_i·u_a)

with dAi the strip area and ua, un the section's chordwise and normal directions (twist and dihedral included). The full local velocity is kept, so sweep, dihedral and sideslip enter directly, and cli(α) may be nonlinear. Newton's method with the analytic Jacobian and a backtracking line search solves the system from the linearised (Prandtl) solution or from the neighbouring angle's solution (sweeps run outward from 0°); a damped fixed-point iteration is the fallback. A point that does not converge is marked converged=False.

Section lift curves: inviscid runs use thin-airfoil theory cl = 2π(α − αL0)/β with α_L0 and cm_c/4 integrated exactly from the piecewise-linear mean line; viscous runs use the section polars (§5) through a monotone cubic (Fritsch–Carlson) on the attached branch, from the angle of cl_min to the angle of cl_max. Beyond the branch the lift is clipped (held at cl_max with zero slope) and the result carries a warning: an integral boundary-layer polar does not predict post-stall lift, so those points are not a stall prediction. Moments add the section cm (c/4) to the moment of the vortex forces. Compressibility enters through the section lift curves only.

5. Viscous strip coupling#

SectionPolars caches 2D viscous polars (solvers.airfoil.polar, the XFOIL-class solver with its ncrit, mach and trip settings) per (airfoil, Reynolds number) and serves interpolated PolarTables:

  • Reynolds number per strip ρ V∞ c / μ with the strip's mean chord. Polars are computed at "nodes": the strips' extreme Reynolds numbers and enough geometric steps between them that two nodes are at most a factor 2 apart; existing nodes are reused whenever they already bracket a strip that closely, so re-analysing a wing (another method, other angles) costs nothing. Strips below 30 % of the largest strip Re (tips) use the polar at that floor (a note says so).
  • Interpolation: linear in α between the computed angles (a 1° grid aligned to multiples of the step, so nodes extend cleanly), linear in log Re between bracketing nodes for cl and cm and linear in log Re of log cd for the drag (exact for a power law cd ∝ Re⁻ⁿ).
  • Range: before any polar runs, an inviscid pass over all requested angles estimates every strip's section angle (thin-airfoil inversion of its lift) and the polars are computed only over that range plus margins (2° below; 2° + 15 % of the angle from zero lift above). When a strip later needs more lift than a polar's computed range reaches while that polar is still rising ("range" rather than "stall" limited), the polar is extended by 3° steps (up to 25°), seeded from its converged edge so the sweep starts from a known boundary-layer state.
  • Stall detection: a table's cl_max is a real stall when the lift falls by more than 0.02 after it or the solver failed above it.
  • Persistence: state() returns the computed points as JSON-safe data and load_state() merges them back (refused when Ncrit, Mach, trips, panels or the angle step differ). Airfoils are keyed by name, so a caller that persists the cache must drop it when a design:<id> outline changes.

Lattice: the strip lift cl_local is inviscid; its profile drag is the section polar's at the angle where the polar gives that lift (the inverse of the attached-branch cl(α); XFLR5 does the same). Strips asked for more than the section cl_max take the drag at cl_max and the result warns that the lattice's linear lift is optimistic there. Lifting line: profile drag and cm at each strip's solved effective angle. In both, the profile-drag force q cd A acts along the free stream at the strip's quarter-chord point (so it contributes to Cm and Cn), and CDv = Σ cdj Aj / Sref, CD = CDi + CDv.

Stall onset (critical section). In viscous runs, the margin of the most loaded strip (lattice: cl_local − cl_max of its section; lifting line: α_eff − α(cl_max)) is followed up the sweep; where it crosses zero, α and CL are interpolated and reported as WingResult.stall_alpha / stall_cl (NaN when the sweep does not reach it, and for inviscid runs), in the notes ("Stall onset … CL ≈ …") and in details["stall"]. This is the classical critical-section estimate of CL_max; on a washed-out wing the stall spreads gradually and the measured CL_max is usually 0–0.1 higher (validation).

Tips. Strips within 5 % of the semispan from a tip are left out of the stall-onset estimate and of the clipping warnings (their loads still count; a note lists angles where only they exceeded cl_max). Strip theory fails where the tip vortex forms, and a rounded tip modelled by sections whose chord falls to zero is the extreme case for a lifting line: the strong trailing leg just inboard of the last, tiny strips puts them in upwash, and their local cl reaches 1.2 where the inboard wing carries 0.45 (NACA TN 1270 planform, α = 6°; the lattice, which resolves the chord, gives 0.39). Without the exclusion those strips would "stall" first at every angle.

Span efficiency and Oswald factor (analysis.wing_metrics). Two different numbers, both reported. The span efficiency e = CL² / (π AR CDi) is per point, from the Trefftz-plane induced drag only (≈ 0.99 for an untwisted taper-0.6 wing). The Oswald factor is the aircraft-design one: fit CD = CD0 + b·CL + k·CL² and take e = 1 / (π AR k), so it also contains the growth of profile drag with lift and is lower (0.7–0.85 for most wings). It depends on the CL range fitted, so the fit is restricted to the linear, attached polar: from the CL of minimum drag up to 0.8 × CL_max (the critical-section stall_cl when known), never at or past stall_alpha, where the lattice keeps strips at cl_max while their lift keeps rising. The range is reported with the figure (oswald_cl_lo / oswald_cl_hi), and with fewer than 4 points or R² < 0.98 the factor is not reported at all. Fitted over a whole −4° to 14° sweep the tapered wing of section 7 gave 0.54, which reads as a very poor wing; over CL 0.10–0.92 it is 0.76. For an inviscid run the fit sees induced drag only and returns the span efficiency (rectangular AR 8: 0.968 vs 0.968).

6. Assumptions and validity limits#

  • Thin wing, potential flow. Thickness affects the solution only through the section polars (lifting line) — the lattice's lift is that of the mean surface. No fuselage, nacelles, gaps, flaps or control-surface deflections; wing–body interference is not modelled.
  • Attached flow. No stall prediction beyond what the section polars contain: the lattice is linear; the lifting line clips each section at its cl_max (warned) and reports the critical-section stall onset. Use CFD for post-stall behaviour.
  • Strip theory for viscous effects. Each strip behaves like a 2D section in the free stream direction: no spanwise boundary-layer flow (relevant on swept wings), no tip-vortex suction on the outer few percent of the span, no influence of the wake on transition. Section data are as good as the 2D solver (Airfoil validation): cd within ~6–15 % of wind-tunnel data, cl_max late and high.
  • Flat, prescribed wake. The wake follows the free stream from the trailing edge; no roll-up, no ground effect.
  • Lifting line: no chordwise loading — over-predicts the lift slope of low-aspect-ratio wings (3–12 % for elliptic wings of AR 12–4, against the lattice) and is unreliable beyond ~20° of sweep (warnings below AR 4 or above 20°).
  • Compressibility: Prandtl–Glauert, subcritical only (M < 0.6 trusted).
  • Reynolds number: the section solver's limits apply (indicative below Re ≈ 5×10⁴; the tip floor above keeps very small tip chords from requesting such polars).

7. Cost#

On the development machine (single process, OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=2): the inviscid lattice (8 × 48 panels) takes ~25 ms per angle and the lifting line ~1 ms per angle. A viscous polar is dominated by the 2D section polars (≈0.5 s per section angle at Re 2–4×10⁵, more near stall): a 17-point polar of a two-section wing needs ~40 section points, ~20–25 s cold and < 1 s once cached (Finite-wing validation, Performance). Section points near and beyond stall cost several times more (Newton retries), so sweeps far past cl_max are slow: the 19-angle (−4° to 14°) polar of a tapered NACA 2412 wing (AR 8.3, strip Re 3.1–5.1×10⁵) computed 52 section points in 26–44 s cold, depending on the load on the machine. A cold viscous wing polar takes under a minute, not seconds; what makes later runs fast is the cache:

  • Reynolds buckets. Section polars are computed at Reynolds-number nodes on a fixed logarithmic grid (48 per decade, 4.9 % apart; every Re is within 2.4 % of a node, inside the 5 % at which a strip uses one node alone). New nodes extend the existing ones greedily, so a study that changes the speed adds only the nodes its new strip Reynolds numbers need: the wing above at 30 m/s after 25 m/s took 9.5 s (22 new points), where the first placement (nodes at both ends of every uncovered range) added two nodes and took 36 s, longer than the cold run.
  • Kept between sessions. simulation.wing writes each cache to the user's configuration folder (cache/section_polars, a few KB) after a run that computed points and reads it back the first time a later session needs it, so a script, the CLI or the assistant (each a new process) re-running a wing analysed before takes ~0.4 s instead of repeating the cold cost. The file key holds the solver settings, Ncrit, Mach, a version and the airfoil fingerprint (project airfoils by a hash of their coordinates); AEROLUME_NO_POLAR_CACHE=1 turns it off and clear_polar_cache() deletes it.
  • Estimates from the cache. section_points_needed(...) plans the first pass of a sweep without computing anything and asks the cache what is missing; simulation.wing.estimate_wing_seconds(design, sim) turns that into a wall time (0.6 s per point, ×1.2 for the polars a sweep extends towards cl_max). Measured on the wing above: cold 34 s estimated / 44 s run, another speed 17 s / 9.5 s, cached 0.9 s / 0.4 s.

8. API#

from aerolume.solvers.wing import analyze_wing, wing_polar, SectionPolars, planform_metrics

sp = SectionPolars(ncrit=9, mach=0.0, coords_provider=lookup)   # one cache per flow condition
res = wing_polar(wing, alphas, speed=20.0, fluid=Fluid(), method="vlm", viscous=True,
                 beta_deg=0.0, moment_ref=None, polar_provider=sp, progress=cb, cancel=event,
                 details=extra)                                  # -> WingResult
pt = analyze_wing(wing, alpha_deg=4.0, speed=20.0, polar_provider=sp)   # -> WingPoint

progress(fraction, message) is reported during the section-polar warm-up (90 % of a cold viscous run) and per angle; cancel returns the points finished so far with a warning; a point that cannot be computed is converged=False with NaN coefficients. Bad inputs (fewer than two sections, non-increasing y, unknown airfoil, Mach ≥ 0.9, |α| ≥ 45°) raise ValueError before any work. details (optional dict) receives stall, strip_y, strip_re, polar_nodes and reference.
