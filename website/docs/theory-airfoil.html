Documentation / Theory

Airfoil analysis (polars): theory and validity

Aerolume's Polar analysis computes lift, drag and moment of a single airfoil section over a range of angles of attack in a few seconds. It is an XFOIL-class viscous–inviscid interaction (VII) method, built on mfoil by K. J. Fidkowski (MIT licence, vendored in src/aerolume/solvers/airfoil/_mfoil.py; provenance and local patches in third_party/mfoil/README.md). This page explains what the method solves, what the knobs mean, and where its results stop being trustworthy. Validation against wind-tunnel data is in Airfoil validation.

For anything that is not a single, attached-or-mildly-separated airfoil (multi-element sections, vehicles, deep stall, bluff bodies, low Reynolds numbers below ~5×10⁴) use the CFD analysis (Navier–Stokes solver (2D)).

1. What is solved#

Outer (inviscid) flow. A linear-vorticity panel method with one panel per pair of surface points (199 panels by default, re-panelled from the user's outline with curvature-based clustering). The stream function is constant on the surface, and the Kutta condition makes the vorticity at the two trailing-edge nodes sum to zero. A blunt trailing edge carries an extra source/vortex panel across the base. The viscous effect on the outer flow enters as transpiration sources σ = d(ue δ*)/ds on the surface and along a wake streamline that leaves the trailing edge, so the edge velocity is

ue = ue_inviscid + D · (ue δ*)        (D = sensitivity matrix of ue to the sources)

Boundary layer. Two-equation integral boundary layer (von Kármán momentum and kinetic-energy shape-parameter equations) on the upper surface, lower surface and wake, with Drela's laminar and turbulent closures (as in XFOIL). The third equation is

  • laminar: the eᴺ envelope amplification equation dn/dξ = f(Hk, Reθ)/θ;
  • turbulent: a lagged shear-stress (Cτ) equation, which lets the turbulent stresses lag the local equilibrium value, important near separation.

Coupling. The unknowns at every node are [θ, δ*, n or √Cτ, ue]. All boundary-layer equations plus the edge-velocity relation above are solved simultaneously by Newton's method, with the transition location and the stagnation point linearised into the system. Solving everything at once is what lets the method handle laminar separation bubbles and trailing-edge separation without the instabilities of "direct" (prescribed-pressure) coupling.

Forces. Lift and moment come from integrating the surface pressure (cm about the quarter chord, positive nose-up). Drag cd comes from the wake momentum thickness far downstream (Squire–Young), which is more accurate than integrating surface pressure; cdf integrates the skin friction and cdp = cd − cdf is the pressure (form) drag.

Compressibility. For Mach > 0 the inviscid pressures get the Kármán–Tsien correction and the boundary-layer closures use the local edge Mach number. This is valid while the flow stays subsonic everywhere; the polar warns when the minimum Cp drops below the critical (sonic) value.

3. Geometry handling (prepare_coords)#

Any outline (Design, Body or point array, any scale, position, orientation or point order) is converted to Selig order (TE → upper → LE → lower → TE) at unit chord:

  • The trailing edge is the end of the longest dimension where the outline turns through most of 180° within ~1 % of the length: a single sharp point, or the two corners of a blunt base (gap up to 10 % of chord; above that the shape is a bluff body and is rejected).
  • The leading edge is the contour point farthest from the TE (XFOIL's definition), refined on a local spline. Exception: an outline already in the design frame (LE at the origin, TE on +x — every Aerolume template and NACA generator) keeps that chord line, so polar angles match the CFD analysis of the same Design. On a cambered nose the two definitions differ slightly (NACA 4412: 0.18° and 3×10⁻⁴ c).
  • Shapes facing downstream are mirrored; multi-body designs, vehicles, rounded or very thick tails (no Kutta condition), self-intersecting outlines, > 50 % thickness or > 25 % camber are rejected with a message starting "Polar analysis needs a single airfoil-like body".

Trailing-edge gap. The viscous solver opens exactly sharp or near-sharp trailing edges (< 0.05 % c) to 0.05 % c, blending the change linearly along the chord (XFOIL's TGAP). With an exactly sharp TE the coupled iteration frequently stalls or converges to a spurious branch; with a 0.05 % gap it converges and the results match the converged sharp-TE ones to ~0.05 % in Cl (measured on NACA 2412, Re 10⁶, 10°). The inviscid panel solution instead closes tiny gaps to an exact sharp TE, because the base source panel is ill-conditioned when it is much shorter than its neighbours.

4. Sweeps, convergence and robustness#

  • Warm-started sweeps. A polar starts with a cold solve at the angle nearest 0°, then walks upwards and downwards from there; every point starts from its converged neighbour, keeping the viscous edge-velocity correction ue − ue_inviscid (XFOIL-style), which roughly halves the Newton iterations per point.
  • Retries. A point that fails is retried with the step split into 2 and 4 sub-steps (intermediate angles), then from a freshly marched boundary layer, then by ramping from 0° in 2° steps. If all fail it is returned with converged = False and NaN coefficients, listed in the result's warnings, and excluded from PolarResult.table(). After three failures in a row (deep stall) each further angle gets a single cold attempt.
  • Convergence. A point is converged when the 2-norm of all boundary-layer residuals is below 10⁻⁸. In a few free-transition cases the transition location flips between two neighbouring panel intervals and the residual settles at ~10⁻³–10⁻² for ever (the amplification-rate onset is so steep that the transition point is not a smooth function of the state). Such a point is accepted when six successive iterates all have ‖R‖ < 0.02 and differ by less than 0.002 in Cl and 1 % in Cd; the polar's notes list these angles.
  • Cancel / progress. polar(..., progress=f, cancel=event) reports f(fraction, message) after each angle and checks the event between Newton iterations; a cancelled sweep returns the points finished so far with a warning.
  • Threads. Each call owns its solver state; there is no shared mutable state, so concurrent calls from worker threads are safe and give bit-identical results to serial calls.

Typical cost on the development machine (numpy/scipy, 2 BLAS threads): 0.3–1 s per converged point; a 19-point NACA 2412 polar at Re 10⁶ took 12.5 s (validation page). Points that need retries (stall, transition hopping) cost several times more. The hot loops run through _fast.py, vectorised re-implementations of mfoil's kernels that are tested against the originals to round-off; the pure-Python originals take ~6 s per point.

5. Validity limits — read before trusting a number#

RegimeBehaviour
Attached flow, Re 2×10⁵–10⁷, M < 0.3Cl within ~0.03–0.05, Cd within ~10–20 % of good wind-tunnel data (see validation). This is the method's home ground.
Laminar separation bubbles (Re ≲ 5×10⁵)modelled (the coupled method captures bubbles), but sensitive to Ncrit and panel density; treat Cd as ±20–30 %.
Re < 5×10⁴warned; bubble bursting and unsteady separation dominate. Use CFD or experiment.
Approaching stall (last few degrees before Cl,max)Cl,max and the stall angle are over-predicted: measured +0.05 to +0.1 on NACA 0012 (Re 3–6×10⁶) and +0.15 to +0.25 on NACA 2412/4412 (Re 6×10⁶), with stall 2–4° late. Integral boundary layers cannot represent massive trailing-edge separation and the wake model assumes a thin wake.
Cambered sections, linear rangelift slightly high: NACA 4412 +0.07 in Cl (≈0.7° in zero-lift angle) against Abbott & von Doenhoff; NACA 2412 within 0.01–0.02. A kink in Cl and Cm appears where the lower-surface transition point runs off the trailing edge (edge of the drag bucket; see below).
Post-stallnot predicted. The method has no physics for large separated regions; points either fail to converge (marked) or return optimistic values. Never use post-stall polars from this solver.
CompressibilityKármán–Tsien correction only; no shocks. Above the critical Mach (warned) results are invalid.
Surface roughness, 3D effects, tunnel wallsnot modelled (roughness only via forced transition). Sections are 2D (infinite span).
Very thin (< 0.5 % t/c), very thick (> 30 % t/c) sectionswarned; leading-edge separation or early separation makes results uncertain.

Ncrit and transition. Free transition is the largest single source of drag uncertainty: the eᴺ method predicts where natural transition would occur in a flow with a given disturbance level, and real surfaces (paint, insects, rivets, rain) usually trip earlier. When in doubt, run the polar twice — free (Ncrit 9) and tripped near the leading edge (xtr ≈ 0.05) — and treat the pair as the bounds of the real drag.

The lift-curve kink at the edge of the drag bucket. As the angle of attack rises, the pressure-side (lower) transition point moves aft and finally off the trailing edge. Over the last 1–3° before it leaves, the laminar boundary layer ahead of it separates a few per cent of chord before the trailing edge (H rises from ~3.5 to 6–7 on NACA 2412 at 3.5°, Re 10⁶). Its displacement thickness acts as extra aft camber: the lift slope rises by up to 50 % and Cm turns nose-down by ~0.01, and both recover once that surface is laminar to the trailing edge. NACA 2412, Re 10⁶, Ncrit 9: dCl/dα = 0.10–0.11 /deg up to 1.5°, 0.137 and 0.153 /deg over 2–4°, then 0.08–0.09 /deg; Cm −0.045 at 2°, −0.060 at 4°, −0.056 at 5°. This is the model's prediction, not a numerical artefact: the pristine mfoil run cold at every angle, and XFOIL 6.99 run on the same coordinates, give the same curve (Cl within 0.006, Cm within 0.001 over 0–6°; validation page). How strongly a real wing section shows it depends on the free-stream disturbance level and surface finish, which Ncrit only approximates; a lower Ncrit or a trip moves or removes it. The polar's notes name the angles where it occurs.
