The Aerolume 3D Navier–Stokes solver — theory, numerics and verification

Module: aerolume.solvers.ns3d (config.py, core.py, poisson.py, geometry.py, fused.py, fields.py, solver.py, rollout.py, dye.py, and for the placed physics of §15–18 physics.py and emitters.py). Verification and validation: validation/ns3d_cases.py, validation/thermal3d_cases.py, validation/bench_ns3d.py; tests: tests/test_ns3d_core.py, tests/test_ns3d_solver.py, tests/test_ns3d_validation.py (-m slow, GPU), tests/test_ns3d_dye.py (smoke, §14), tests/test_ns3d_physics.py (heat transfer, fans, ports, §15 and §17), tests/test_ns3d_emitters.py (§16), tests/test_ns3d_live.py (§18) and tests/test_ns3d_physics_validation.py (-m slow).

The 3D solver is the validated 2D solver (Navier–Stokes solver (2D)) carried to three dimensions: the same equations, staggered grid, time integrator, exact projection, immersed boundary with the same calibrated wall offset, the same force evaluation and the same differentiable functional core. This document states what is new in 3D, why, and what was measured. Every number was produced on the development machine (RTX 4060 Ti 8 GB, i7-13700F, torch 2.14.1+cu130, Windows 11) by the scripts above; the GPU was shared with other work during most runs, so throughputs and wall times are lower bounds on what an idle machine does (the exception is the cost table of §18, measured on an idle GPU and checked for contention).

1. Equations, frame and coefficients#

∂u/∂t + ∇·(u u)=−∇p + (1/Re) ∇²u + ∇·(2 νt S) − (χ/η)(u − us)
∇·u=0

non-dimensional with the reference length L (sphere diameter, vehicle length, mean aerodynamic chord), the free-stream speed U, time L/U and pressure ρU²; Re = UL/ν. Frame (DESIGN 7.2): right-handed, x downstream, y to the left, z up; the flow enters at x = x0 along +x. Attitude is applied to the geometry by the planner, so the solver's forces are wind-axis forces: Fx drag, Fy side force, Fz lift. Moments about moment_ref are the components of M = Σ (r − rref) × F: Mx roll, My pitch (positive nose-up: a positive rotation about +y lifts the nose, which points to −x), Mz yaw. Coefficients:

CD, CS, CL=(Fx, Fy, Fz) / (½ refarea)Cm = My / (½ refarea lengthref)
Croll, Cn=(Mx, Mz) / (½ refarea spanref)

(ρU² = 1 in solver units). A lift force behind the moment reference gives a negative (nose-down) Cm, as in aeronautics; a test pitches a plate nose-up by +10° about +y and checks that CL > 0.

Symmetry plane. With bc_y = "symmetry_ymin" the domain is the half y ≥ y0 of a mirror-symmetric problem and the plane y = y0 is a free-slip wall — for incompressible flow a free-slip wall is a symmetry plane (v = 0, ∂u/∂y = ∂w/∂y = 0, ∂p/∂y = 0), so the numerics are those of a slip wall. The solver reports full-body values: summing the half-body forces with their mirror images (y → 2y0 − y, Fy → −Fy) gives exactly

Fx = 2 Fx_half,  Fz = 2 Fz_half,  My = 2 My_half,  Fy = 0,
Mx = −(yr − y0) Fz,  Mz = (yr − y0) Fx       (zero when the moment reference lies on the plane)

3. Spatial discretisation#

Advection is the 2D conservative form in three directions: for each momentum component the flux along its own direction lives at cell centres (advecting velocity = the component itself) and the two cross fluxes on the cell edges, with the advecting velocity averaged to the edge (e.g. the y-flux of u-momentum at the x–y edges carries (vi−½ + vi+½)/2). Each flux is the 2D one — central (kinetic-energy conserving with a discretely divergence-free velocity) or the default upwind3 (4th-order central part plus a |V|-weighted 4th-difference dissipation, the 3rd-order upwind-biased reconstruction). Diffusion is the 7-point Laplacian (ν ∇²u, valid since ∇·u = 0).

Verification — Taylor–Green. The 2D Taylor–Green vortex is an exact Navier–Stokes solution in 3D as well; it is set up in each coordinate plane in turn (so every pair of velocity components and every pair of axes is exercised), periodic, Re = 10, t = 0.5, max velocity error:

4. Pressure: exact projection with orthonormal transforms#

Each Runge–Kutta stage ends with L ψ = ∇h·û, u = û − ∇h ψ, L the 7-point Laplacian with homogeneous Neumann conditions (or periodic) per axis. L is a Kronecker sum Lx ⊕ Ly ⊕ Lz of 1D operators whose eigenvectors are known: the DCT-II basis for cell-centred Neumann, and for periodic axes the discrete Hartley basis cas(2πkj/n)/√n (cas = cos + sin) — real, symmetric, orthonormal, and an eigenbasis because the periodic Laplacian is a symmetric circulant (λk = λn−k). With the orthonormal matrices Q (rows = eigenvectors) a solve is three forward transforms, a division by λx + λy + λz and three inverse transforms with Qᵀ. The constant mode is set to zero.

Why matrix products instead of FFT-based DCTs (the 2D solver uses Makhoul's packed DCT): on the grids this solver runs (≤ ~600 cells per axis) a transform along one axis is a single cuBLAS GEMM that reads and writes the field once — along x (nz·ny, nx) @ Qᵀ, along y a batched (ny × ny) @ (ny × nx), along z Q @ (nz, ny·nx) — with no transposes, no complex arithmetic and nothing to repack. Measured at 256 × 128 × 128 in float32: 0.9 ms for a forward + inverse 3D transform, against 0.8 ms for a bare rfftn + irfftn (which would still need the DCT pre- and post-processing). The x transform is fed the pre-transposed matrix: cuBLAS's transposed-operand kernel was up to 4× slower there. The cost per element grows like n, so very long axes would favour FFTs again; at 11.8 M cells (384 × 192 × 160) a solve takes 3.7 ms.

TF32 must never be used here: its 10-bit mantissa would leave a divergence of ~10⁻³. Every solve runs inside ieee_matmul(), which forces IEEE float32 for cuBLAS (and oneDNN on the CPU) through PyTorch's per-backend fp32_precision switch and restores the previous setting; a GPU test enables TF32 process-wide and checks that the projected divergence stays below 10⁻⁵.

Verification: max|Lψ − f|/max|f| 4.9 × 10⁻¹⁴ in float64 (Neumann, periodic and mixed, even and odd sizes), 2.2 × 10⁻⁵ in float32 on the GPU (256 × 128 × 128). Projected divergence of real solver states (sphere, moving road, no-slip top): 7.3 × 10⁻¹⁶ (float64), 4.0 × 10⁻⁷ (float32, GPU); the bars are 10⁻¹⁰ and 10⁻⁵. As in 2D the pressure written to the state is the step-averaged p = (⅙ψ₁ + ⅔ψ₂ + ψ₃)/Δt, the pressure of the discrete momentum balance (Navier–Stokes solver (2D), §4).

5. Time integration and time step#

SSP-RK3 with a projection after every stage, exactly as in 2D. The time step is Δt = min(cfl·h/(max|u| + max|v| + max|w|), h²/(6(1/Re + max νt))), cfl = 0.5: the sum of the three component maxima bounds the 3D advective CFL number, and the viscous limit keeps the 2D solver's 80 % margin (SSP-RK3 is stable to ≈ 2.51 on the negative real axis and the 7-point Laplacian reaches 12ν/h², so the limit is 0.209 h²/ν). Δt is a device scalar computed every step; nothing in a step synchronises with the host.

6. Immersed bodies#

6.1 Masks from triangle meshes#

Bodies are triangle soups (ImmersedMesh), not necessarily closed or consistently wound. The solid fraction is the 2D compact C¹ half-sine step of the signed distance d (negative inside), shifted δ = 0.32 h inwards, half-width w = h, evaluated directly at the u-, v- and w-face positions and at cell centres, on a window of cells around the bodies (plus a margin of w + 3 + δ cells, clipped to the domain; boundary faces are never penalised).

The signed distance comes from aerolume.geometry3d.voxel.sdf_band when that module is importable: triangles binned to cells, the exact point–triangle distance within the band (2.32 h here, enough for the ramp), the sign from ray-parity voting along three axes, which is robust to holes, randomly flipped triangles and overlapping parts, and a differentiable recompute of the distance from the selected triangle. The solver evaluates it directly on its window, which the domain clips — always at a symmetry plane, often at the road. The voxeliser once classified a face whose probes left its grid as an open sheet and thickened it by half a cell (distances up to 0.45 h too small next to the plane), so this bridge evaluated a grid covering the whole body; the voxeliser now grows its own classification field past such a cut, and a cut window equals a covering grid to rounding (tests: half domains, windows across an open mesh, open tyres through the road, a sheet across the plane). A grid point exactly on a face — every row of centres on an axis-aligned face at round coordinates — takes the face's signed plane distance, so the mask keeps its derivative there (§11).

Without the shared module a private exact implementation runs: the nearest triangle by chunked brute force (no autograd), the distance recomputed from it with autograd, and the sign from the generalised winding number (|w| > ½; van Oosterom & Strackee solid angles). It tolerates small holes and a globally flipped mesh but not inconsistent winding, and its cost is points × triangles; it agrees with the shared one to 2 × 10⁻⁸ on a sphere (test). It also uses the signed plane distance within 0.02 cells of a face.

The wall calibration carries over unchanged. The 2D values η = min(0.05 h, h² Re/60) and δ = 0.32 h were calibrated on plane Poiseuille flow between penalised slabs — a 1D property of the discretisation normal to a grid-aligned wall, identical in 3D. The sphere results of §10 (control-volume closure to 0.01–0.03 %, drag converging to the reference values once the confinement is accounted for) are its independent 3D validation. The mask volume of a sphere equals the radial integral of the shifted ramp to 0.5 % (the ramp's first moment adds ≈ 0.19 · 4π r w² to the volume of its 0.5 iso-surface; test).

Rigid rotation (wheels) enters as χ us with us = ω a × (x − c) about an arbitrary axis a, pre-multiplied into the window arrays. Sign: with a = +y the point below the axle moves at −ωr in x, so a wheel rolling on a road moving at +U has ω = −U/r (the 2D counter-clockwise +U/r seen from the −y side); a test checks the solid velocity at the contact point.

6.2 Penalisation, forces and moments#

Implicit Brinkman penalisation per stage, u ← (u + kχus)/(1 + kχ), k = bs Δt/η (2D §6.2). The force on each body is the momentum the penalisation removes, F = −(h³/Δt) Σ wb (⅙Δ₁ + ⅔Δ₂ + Δ₃) (wb the body's share of χ), with the moment lever arms taken at each face's own position: u-faces carry Fx and contribute (z − zr)Fx to My and −(y − yr)Fx to Mz, v-faces Fy (−(z − zr)Fy to Mx, (x − xr)Fy to Mz), w-faces Fz ((y − yr)Fz to Mx, −(x − xr)Fz to My). Tests: a sphere has zero side force, lift and moments to 10⁻³ of its drag; moments about two references differ exactly by (r₁ − r₂) × F (10⁻¹⁰); several bodies sum; a half-domain run returns the full-domain coefficients to 10⁻⁹ (float64, a box on the symmetry plane).

Control-volume cross-check. For a steady flow the drag also follows from the momentum balance on a box around the body, Fx = −∮ [p nx + u (u·n) − ν(∂u/∂xj + ∂uj/∂x) nj] dA, every quantity taken at second order from its own staggered position (ns3d_cases.control_volume_drag; on the symmetry plane the box has no face — v = 0 and ∂u/∂y = 0 there — and the result is doubled). On the sphere it matches the penalisation impulse to 0.01–0.03 % at every Reynolds number and resolution of §10, while its pressure, momentum-flux and viscous parts change by tens of per cent between a half- and a full-domain box: the impulse is the force of the discrete solution.

6.3 Surface pressure and friction#

FlowSolver3D.surface(mean) returns, per mesh vertex, cp, the wall-shear vector tau (scaled as a skin-friction coefficient, tau = (2/Re) ∂ut/∂n) and cf = |tau|, sampled exactly as in 2D: along the vertex normal (area-weighted) at probes d₁ = 1.5 h and d₂ = 2.5 h off the wall, cp extrapolated linearly to the wall, ∂ut/∂n from the quadratic through the wall velocity (zero, or ω a × r on a wheel) and the tangential velocity at both probes, every quantity trilinear from its own staggered grid. Normals are oriented per vertex towards the fluid (the side where the solid fraction at the first probe is lower), so any winding works; on a sheet with fluid on both sides the mesh's own orientation is kept. In a half-domain run the probes of vertices on the missing half are sampled at their mirror image (v reversed), so the full mesh gets mirror-symmetric data (test: identical to 10⁻¹² at mirror vertices).

As in 2D the surface integral of −cp n + tau is a consistency check, not the force: the probes under-resolve the wall gradient when the boundary layer spans few cells. On the sphere it is 13 / 7.6 / 4.0 % low at Re 100 and 16 / 24 / 32 cells/D (§10, almost all of it friction).

7. Subgrid models (3D LES)#

turbulence = "smagorinsky" | "wale" | "vreman" adds ∇·(2νt S) with ν_t at cell centres (with a ghost ring) from the velocity-gradient tensor — diagonal components from face differences, off-diagonal ones the average of the four surrounding edge differences — diagonal stresses at centres, shear stresses on edges with ν_t averaged from the four cells around each edge: conservative and dissipative. Constants as in 2D (Cs = 0.17, Cw = 0.5, c = 0.07, filter width h). The models are evaluated on the traceless gradient (g₃₃ := −g₁₁ − g₂₂): identical in the interior, where the discrete divergence is zero to round-off after every projection, and it leaves eight independent components, which lets the fused path evaluate a model in one kernel. ν_t is zero inside solids (× (1 − χc)) and its maximum enters the viscous time-step limit. WALE and Vreman vanish in pure shear (laminar boundary layers); Smagorinsky does not (tests). Unlike 2D, these are genuine 3D LES models — but on the grids an 8 GB GPU affords, wall-bounded flows at vehicle Reynolds numbers are wall-unresolved LES (§12).

8. Start-up perturbation#

perturb = True adds a Lamb–Oseen vortex with its axis along y (peak swirl 5 % of U, core radius 0.75 × the body height), confined spanwise by a Gaussian, centred on the bodies and projected. It is divergence-free for any spanwise envelope (∂u/∂x + ∂w/∂z = 0 and v = 0), it breaks the up–down symmetry so an unsteady wake locks in promptly, and it is mirror-symmetric about the y = y0 plane when that is a symmetry plane, so half- and full-domain runs evolve identically. Steady validation cases run with perturb = False.

9. Performance#

A 3D step written as plain torch operations is bound by memory traffic: each upwind3 flux costs about nine elementwise passes over a field and a step evaluates 27 fluxes, plus padding, diffusion, projection and bookkeeping. That reference core (core.py, kept as the differentiable path and the CPU path) runs 256 × 128 × 128 at 10.7–13.1 steps/s on the GPU. There is no Triton and no CUDA compiler on the development machine, so torch.compile and custom extensions are out; PyTorch's jiterator (torch.cuda.jiterator) compiles small elementwise CUDA kernels at run time through NVRTC, which ships with PyTorch, and accepts strided views as inputs. A whole stencil — five shifted views of the padded velocity and the edge velocity sums — is then one kernel that reads each array once (neighbouring reads hit the L2 cache) and writes once: a 7-input stencil costs about one contiguous elementwise pass (0.16 ms at 4.2 M cells). The fused path (fused.py) uses, per component and stage:

  • crossd0 / crossd: advection along each cross direction (both edges, precomputed edge velocity sums) plus the diffusion along that direction;
  • updself: advection and diffusion along the component's own direction fused with the Runge–Kutta update lerp(uprev, u0, a) + bΔt L, so the stage value never needs its own pass;
  • div and gsub for the projection (ψ padded once with replicated or wrapped ghosts, which makes the gradient on Neumann boundary faces exactly zero without slice writes);
  • for LES, 18 kernels: eight gradient components, the eddy viscosity of the model in one kernel (eight inputs thanks to the traceless gradient), edge and diagonal stresses, and their divergence accumulated into the tendencies.

jiterator takes at most eight tensor inputs, which is what shapes the split; boundary faces are fixed after the fused update. The viscosity is a kernel launch argument, so a captured CUDA graph bakes it in and set_inflow re-captures (the reference core reads the nu tensor). The fused step equals the reference to 3 × 10⁻¹⁴ after 20 steps (moving road, WALE) and < 10⁻¹¹ in the tests in float64 for every boundary-condition and subgrid combination (tests), and FlowSolver3D runs the same CUDA-graph machinery as 2D (blocks of 10 steps captured after a side-stream warm-up, device-side CFL, history ring, live edits into existing tensors); graph and eager runs are bit-identical (test). Any compile, capture or replay failure falls back to the reference core or eager execution with a warning.

Running means use a weighted Welford update in float32 (mean and the sum of squared deviations, which keeps the variance accurate where a sum of squares would cancel), half the memory of float64 accumulators. They sample the flow once every 10 steps with the elapsed time as the weight — a rectangle rule over ~0.1 L/U, far below any flow time scale — on the same steps in graph and eager runs, and reading them folds in the time since the last sample. This cut the averaging overhead from 20 % to ~4 % of a step.

11. Differentiability#

step3d(state, geom, ops, dt) is pure torch with static control flow; everything between the mesh vertices and the force coefficients is differentiable: the signed distance (recomputed from the nearest triangle with autograd, in both the shared and the private implementation) → C¹ mask → penalisation → projection (matrix products) → force impulse. rollout3d advances n steps with torch.utils.checkpoint over chunks of k steps and a fixed Δt. Gradient check (float64, CPU, 20 × 12 × 12 cells, an 80-triangle sphere at 5 cells/D, Re 20, 10 steps, J = mean CD, a smooth vertex-displacement mode — stretch along x and half that along z): autograd 9.1521 against 9.1521 from central differences (ε = 10⁻⁵), relative error 2.9 × 10⁻¹⁰ with the private exact distance and 2.8 × 10⁻¹⁰ with the shared voxeliser. Gradients w.r.t. the inflow and the initial state are non-zero and finite, and LES steps are differentiable including w.r.t. the cell-centre solid fraction (tests). The fused kernels have no autograd; FlowSolver3D uses them only under torch.no_grad.

The distance is differentiable through the surface as well: a grid point exactly on a face (or within the inside test's 0.002-cell ray jitter of it) takes the face's signed plane distance, whose sign comes from the face's outward side, instead of ±√(d²) — which has no derivative at d = 0 and whose sign the jittered parity can get wrong there — and a point exactly as far from two faces (a medial plane through a row of centres, a box edge) takes the mean of their derivatives, the one central differences measure for a two-way tie. Before, an axis-aligned box with faces on grid planes gave a discontinuous objective and gradient errors of 25–30 % (median over modes) in a direct check of the masks; now 10⁻⁴ or better (tests/test_geometry3d_voxel.py, and Sensitivity maps, §9.4 for the adjoint).

14. Passive smoke: flow visualisation in the volume#

Users asked to see the flow around the body in 3D -- smoke and streamlines in space, not only colour on the surface (DESIGN.md 7.13). Streamlines need nothing from the solver beyond the velocity it already stores; smoke does: the solver transports a passive scalar c (0 = clean air, 1 = undiluted smoke) released by emitters upstream of the body, the 2D dye carried to 3D (solvers/ns3d/dye.py; FlowSolver3D.field("dye")).

Equation and placement. dc/dt + div(u c) = 0, no physical diffusion (smoke particles follow the flow; the numerics add the only spreading). c is cell-centred on the MAC grid, so the face velocities the solver already holds are exactly the advecting velocities of a finite-volume flux: no interpolation. With the end-of-step velocity, which is discretely divergence-free after the projection, a uniform c stays uniform to round-off and the total smoke is conserved exactly away from emitters, solids and the outlet.

Scheme. MUSCL reconstruction with the van Leer limiter (the harmonic mean of the one-sided differences, zero at extrema) and one forward Euler step per solver step; the face value is taken from the upwind side. A forward Euler step with a limited slope is total-variation diminishing when the sum of the face Courant numbers is at most 1 / (1 + psi_max / 2), which is 1/2 for van Leer (psi_max = 2). The solver's time step is cfl h / (max|u| + max|v| + max|w|) with cfl = 0.5 (§5), exactly that bound, so the scheme creates no new extrema; the update is then clipped to [0, 1] (an unsplit TVD bound is sharp; round-off excursions are removed, not allowed to grow) and values below 10⁻⁸ are set to exactly zero -- invisible, and on x86 CPUs the limiter's decaying tails would otherwise run into subnormal floats (~100× slower). First order in time is a deliberate choice: smoke is a picture, not a measurement, and one update per step keeps the cost to three flux passes and one update pass.

Boundaries and solids. Clean air enters at the inlet (zero ghost cells); the outlet is zero-gradient; walls, the road and the symmetry plane mirror the field (their zero normal velocity makes their flux zero anyway; the mirror only keeps the limiter's slopes symmetric, so a half-domain run gives the exact mirror half of the full run); periodic axes wrap. Cells whose solid fraction exceeds one half are set to zero after every step, so the field is zero inside bodies. The Brinkman velocity there is ~0, so almost no smoke flows in: this removes what numerical spreading puts into the diffuse interface rather than acting as a sink for smoke that hugs the surface.

Emitters (DyeRake: "rake" a row of nozzles across the span, "grid" a span × height grid, "plane" an emitting band). After each step the smoke inside a nozzle is relaxed to 1, c += s (1 - c), with a nozzle weight s that is 1 inside the radius and falls linearly to 0 over one cell (anti-aliased: a nozzle between cell centres still emits, and moving it by a fraction of a cell moves its stream). A nozzle is two cells deep along x. Optional on/off pulses cut the streams into time lines; the pulse switch is evaluated on the device from the solver time and the pulse parameters live in a tensor, so neither ever leaves a CUDA graph. The default radius is 1.25 cells (a stream ~2.5 cells across, the thinnest the limiter carries over a body length at Draft resolution) and nozzles are about six radii apart (at least 4.8 once the count is rounded), so the streams stay distinct. The default rake (the GUI's, viz3d.flow.resolve_rake, and the solver's own default_dye_rake) stands 0.4 of the body's smaller dimension upstream of the nose, spans the body plus 20 % on each side and sits at 40 % of its height, where the streams split at the nose -- over the top, round the sides, under the body -- and roll up in the wake.

Fast path. On CUDA the update runs as fused jiterator kernels (fused.dye_flux, one per direction, reading the four-cell stencil as strided views of the padded field; fused.dye_update) inside the same CUDA graphs as the flow step; the smoke buffer is part of the graphs' static state. Before using them the solver compiles them and compares one step with the reference scheme (check_dye_kernels); a failure falls back to the plain-torch reference with dye_error set. The smoke is solver state, not part of the differentiable State3D: it never feeds back into the flow, so shape sensitivities never need it.

  • Results (simulation/runner3d.py): the result keeps an instantaneous snapshot at the end of the run on the stored volume's block grid -- the smoke and the velocity of that moment (CFD3DResult.extra_volumes, label "instant"; an average of smoke is a blur, not a picture) -- next to the mean velocity, so a result shows the smoke, the mean-flow streamlines and the instantaneous ones (which, in a shedding wake, look like the smoke and not like the mean).
  • Rendering (viz3d.Scene.add_smoke): GPU ray casting (vtkSmartVolumeMapper) of the smoke through a fixed transfer function -- transparent below c = 0.02, then a convex opacity rise per voxel (the faint haze stays clear and the cores carry the picture), softly shaded by the scene's lights, near-white on the dark theme and dark grey on the light one; fixed sample distance and no jittering, so a developing run does not flicker. Tubes are 0.18 % of the lines' extent in radius, at most a fifth of the seed spacing, so neighbouring lines never merge.

15. Heat transfer and buoyancy (DESIGN.md 10.2)#

Everything in §15–18 is off by default. A configuration without heat transfer, fans, ports, diagnostics or the PID time step builds no physics object (SolverConfig3D.has_placed_physics) and runs the base step: the default path is bit-identical to the solver without placed physics (laminar and WALE, eager and CUDA graphs, 57 steps of a sphere with smoke and running means: largest difference of every field, the history and the means exactly 0) and launches the same CUDA kernels (2176 per 5 steps, the same multiset of kernel names under torch.profiler), so it costs exactly what it cost before. Emitters (§16) run in the smoke step, which is part of the default path.

15.1 Equations and units#

The temperature is carried as θ = (T − T_amb) / ΔT_ref, cell-centred like the pressure:

∂θ/∂t + ∇·(u θ) = ∇·((1/Pe + ν_t/Pr_t) ∇θ) + q,      Pe = Re Pr,  Pr_t = 0.85
∂u/∂t + …       = … + Ri θ e_z                         (gravity along −z, Boussinesq)

with Ri = g β ΔT_ref L / U². Heat quantities cross the solver's interface in conduction units k ΔT_ref L (a heat rate Q means Q k ΔT_ref L watts), so a body's mean Nusselt number on the reference length is Nu = Q / (A θ_b) with A its area in L². Natural convection in a closed box uses the free-fall scaling U = √(gβΔT L): Re = √(Ra/Pr), Pe = √(Ra Pr), Ri = 1 (thermal3d_cases.cavity_config). The planner chooses ΔT_ref and the scaling and converts the SI model types (DESIGN.md 10.2).

15.2 Discretisation#

  • Coupling. θ is advanced inside the same SSP-RK3 stages as the momentum, with the same (a, b) pairs and the stage's discretely divergence-free face velocities, so transport and buoyancy are coupled at the scheme's order and a uniform θ stays uniform to round-off.
  • Fluxes. Advection is the smoke's MUSCL / van Leer face value (§14: bounded and second order where smooth) times the face velocity; diffusion is the 7-point flux form κ (θ_c − θ_b)/h. The update is the divergence of face fluxes, so the scheme is conservative: the heat that enters through walls, bodies, sources and jets is accounted for exactly (§15.5). With an LES model the face diffusivity is κ + (ν_t,L + ν_t,R)/(2 Pr_t), molecular on non-periodic boundary faces (the wall rules use the molecular value).
  • Time step: a combined bound, not the minimum of two. A forward-Euler stage of the MUSCL / van Leer advection is TVD for a Courant sum C ≤ ½ (§14) and the 7-point diffusion is positive for 6D ≤ 1 (D = κ_eff dt/h²); together the stage is a convex combination of neighbouring values — bounded, no new extrema — only when C/½ + 6D ≤ 1, and SSP-RK3 keeps the property because its stages are convex combinations of such steps. The heat equation therefore limits the step to dt ≤ 1 / (Σmax|u_i| / (½ h) + κ_eff / (h²/6)) on top of the momentum's own limits (§5). The first implementation took min(CFL limit, diffusion limit) instead, which lets C = 0.49 and 6D = 1 occur together: a heated sphere at 24 cells per diameter (Re 50, Pr 0.71, where the two limits coincide) grew an oscillation ×1.13 per step from step ~120 until θ overflowed, while the drag was unaffected (Ri = 0). The regression test is the worst case — uniform flow with κ = hU/3, so both limits coincide exactly, and a checkerboard θ: the old rule amplified it 10⁷ in 25 steps; the combined rule keeps θ inside its initial range to 10⁻¹² (a discrete maximum principle). The price is a smaller step where advection and diffusion are both near their limits (Pe ~ 1 per cell): at most half the minimum of the two — 55 % more steps for the heated sphere at Re 50 and 16 cells per diameter (4250 against 2750) — and nothing where one effect dominates. The 2D solver arrived at the same bound independently (Navier–Stokes solver (2D); a heated cylinder reached 10³⁰ there).
  • From rest. In still air the CFL rule sees no velocity, so two more limits apply (also as in 2D): the momentum sources' speed scale (jet speeds, a regulated fan's target, √(T/A) or √Δp of a thrust or pressure-jump fan, times 1 + |swirl|) enters the CFL bound, and a parcel at rest, which accelerates at Ri |θ|, may move at most cfl cells in one step: dt ≤ cfl √(h / (Ri max|θ|)). Neither binds in any validation case of §15.6 or §17 (checked); they matter for fans, jets and hot bodies started in still air at high Re (test: first step within both).
  • Domain sides enter through two ghost layers: "temperature" 2θ_w − θ (mirror about the wall value), "adiabatic" and the symmetry plane the even mirror, "heat_flux" the mirror plus G h (first layer) and 3 G h (second), so the face gradient is exactly −∂θ/∂n = G, a tunnel inlet carries θ_inlet and the outlet is zero-gradient; periodic axes wrap. A thermal condition set on a side that is an inlet, outlet or symmetry plane is ignored with a note.
  • Fused path. On CUDA the face fluxes and the stage update run as jiterator kernels (fused.th_flux_s with κ/h as a launch value, th_flux_t with an LES diffusivity tensor, th_upd), inside the step's CUDA graphs. Before use they are compiled and compared with the reference arithmetic (check_theta_kernels); a failure falls back to plain torch for θ only, with theta_error set.

15.3 Bodies, and why buoyancy is added after the penalisation#

  • "temperature" bodies are penalised implicitly towards their temperature with the momentum's mask and calibrated wall offset: θ ← (θ + k χ θ_b)/(1 + k χ), k = b dt/η_T, η_T = min(0.05 h, h² Pe/60) — the momentum rule (Navier–Stokes solver (2D), §6.3) with Pe for Re. The heat the penalisation adds, weighted per stage like the momentum increments (1/6, 2/3, 1), is the body's heat-transfer rate, exactly as its momentum impulse is its force; with several bodies each one's share is its fraction of the overlap.
  • "adiabatic" bodies block conduction: the diffusive flux through a face is multiplied by (1 − χ_face). Inside an insulating slab that spans the box, the flux through its central face is 0 to 10⁻¹⁵ (test). The interior of an adiabatic body is then isolated; its temperature is whatever it was.
  • "heat_flux" bodies release G = q″L/(kΔT) through a skin: the derivative of the unshifted smooth mask (a cosine delta one mask width across, centred on the surface), normalised over a grid holding the whole body so that the released heat is exactly G·A (cut by the domain: the in-domain part). Their wall temperature is measured in that skin — the release-weighted mean of θ over the cells the heat goes into (ThermalGeometry.wall_theta), recorded every step as theta_wall_<i> — and their Nusselt number is Q/(A θ_w) from it (§15.7 says why the earlier probe extrapolation was wrong).
  • Buoyancy. Ri θ is added to each stage value after the penalisation and everywhere, also inside solids; the fictitious fluid inside a solid then feels buoyancy too, and the momentum balance of the body's volume gives the force on the body as the penalisation impulse minus Ri ∫θ dV over the body (with a volume-accurate indicator, below). This is a deliberate change from the first implementation, which switched buoyancy off inside the diffuse solid and applied it before the penalisation. Measured on a fixed insulating sphere in a linearly stratified, non-conducting fluid (the exact answer: the fluid at rest, F_z = −Ri θ_c V), that formulation had no discrete rest state — spurious currents of 1–2 % of the buoyancy velocity — and gave 62–79 % of the Archimedes force at 6.4–9.6 cells per diameter, because (1 − χ) Ri θ is not a gradient across the interface and the penalisation acts on the predictor before the projection removes the gradient part. Added after the penalisation (like the pressure, which balances most of it), a linear stratification is an exact discrete equilibrium. The force correction uses the smoothed indicator without the calibrated wall offset (ThermalGeometry.vol_w): the momentum mask sits ~0.25 h inside the surface and under-counts the volume by ~20 % at 6 cells per diameter, while the unshifted one integrates to the true volume at second order. The fluid away from solids sees the same arithmetic as before: the cavity results of §15.6, re-run at two grids per Rayleigh number after this change and the time-step change of §15.2, agree with the earlier runs to all printed digits.

Heat sources and the step from rest (review round 5 before the release). 15.7 Heat-flux bodies: the wall temperature and the Nusselt number#

A body with a set heat flux knows its heat rate exactly (Q = G A, §15.3); its Nusselt number Nu = Q/(A θ_w) is only as good as the wall temperature θ_w. Until this round θ_w came from surface(): θ at probes 1.5 h and 2.5 h off the wall, extrapolated linearly to it. That needs the thermal boundary layer to span both probes. At Draft resolution a body metres long at a few m/s has a boundary layer of about one cell, both probes sit in nearly ambient fluid, and the extrapolation returns almost the ambient temperature. The review's case — a 0.5 m box at 200 W/m², 1 m/s, Re 3.4·10⁴, 30 cells per length — reported Q = 156 W (exactly flux × area) but Nu 1,821, i.e. a 2 K wall excess, while the colour map showed 13 K warm fluid along the wall. The heat rate was right; the temperature it was divided by was not.

The measurement now. The skin of a heat-flux body is where the model puts the wall's heat: a cosine delta one mask width either side of the surface. ThermalGeometry.wall_theta takes the mean of θ over those cells, weighted like the release (a gather of ~10³ cells per body, recorded every step as theta_wall_<i>; the per-step nu then covers heat-flux bodies too). At a resolved boundary layer it agrees with θ sampled on the surface and with the probe extrapolation (table: "surface", "probe" columns); unlike the probes it stays near the wall temperature when the layer is one or two cells thick. surface() reports θ sampled on the surface itself for heat-flux bodies (the per-vertex map), and heat_transfer(), heat_rates() and physics_history() all use the skin temperature.

15.8 When buoyancy dominates: the time step of a Ri ≫ 1 run#

A heated body in a slow stream is a plume, not a wake. The review's case — a 0.1 m sphere at 60 °C in 20 °C air at Re 100 (U = 0.0146 m/s) — has Ri = gβΔT L/U² = 627 and Gr = 6.3·10⁶: the buoyant velocity U_b = √(gβΔT L) = U √Ri is 25 times the stream. 16. Emitters: smoke channels and tracer particles (DESIGN.md 10.1)#

Smoke channels. Up to four passive scalars — fields "dye", "dye1", "dye2", "dye3" — each transported exactly like the base solver's smoke (§14: bounded, zero in solids, conserved away from sources). An Emitter3D of species "dye" (solver units; the planner converts the model's metres, seconds, colours to channels and the wind / body frame through Emitter3D.scaled) relaxes its channel towards rate inside its footprint after every step, c += s·on·(rate − c), with s the anti-aliased footprint weight and on the release switch: enabled, start ≤ t < stop, and the first pulse_duty of every pulse_period. The switch is evaluated on the device from the solver time and a per-emitter parameter tensor, so pulses, start and stop never leave a CUDA graph and editing them never re-captures. Footprints: "point" (a nozzle of radius, default 1.25 cells, two cells deep along normal), "line" (a capsule), "rake" (count nozzles), "rect" (an axis-aligned box; a zero extent is a two-cell slab), "disc" (radius size[0], facing normal), "plane" (a rectangle facing normal, or the whole cross-section). emitters=None keeps the base automatic rake on channel 0 (the default path), emitters=[] releases nothing; a jet's dye_channel adds a channel too. Cost: one more cell-centred field and one fused advection (§14, ~6 % of a step) per extra channel.

Tracer particles live in fixed device buffers (position, alive, birth, nozzle), one ring of particle_capacity / n (default 2¹⁶) slots per particle emitter. Every step an emitter adds rate·dt·on to an accumulator and releases its integer part — at most k_max per step, a static size (k_max = ⌈4 rate dt_estimate⌉, at most a quarter of the ring), so the release is graph-capturable; the candidate slots are always k_max long and those beyond the count keep their contents. "point" and "rake" particles leave from their nozzle centres (a rake's nozzles take turns, so each carries the same share; within radius when one is given), area kinds from uniformly random points of their footprint. The releases of a step are spread evenly over it in time: the j-th of n leaves at t − f_j dt with f_j = (j + ½)/n and moves only for f_j dt (its full-step displacement is scaled), so a nozzle draws an evenly spaced streak line at any release rate and age = t − birth is the time each has moved. (The first version stamped the step's end time on particles that were then moved through the whole step, so every age was one dt short, and scattered a nozzle's releases over its two-cell depth, which made streak lines zig-zag by ±1 cell for 0.08 L of advance per step; both found by the tests and fixed.) Particles are advected with the midpoint rule through the trilinearly interpolated staggered velocity of the step's final, divergence-free state, and retired when they leave the domain, enter a solid (solid fraction > ½, bodies and paint) or outlive lifetime; the ring overwrites its oldest particles (recycling); a symmetry plane reflects them (the velocity is sampled at the mirror image, v reversed); periodic axes wrap. FlowSolver3D.particles() returns positions, age, birth, emitter and nozzle; particle_lines() one polyline per nozzle of every point / rake emitter, ordered from the nozzle outwards and cut where a pulsed emitter paused (its dashes line up into timelines across a rake).

CPU cost. Graph capture needs static shapes, so a step advects every slot of the ring, alive or not: free on a GPU, but on the CPU a 2¹⁶ ring holding 494 particles cost 412 ms per step against 27.8 ms for the flow step of the 32 × 12 × 12 test tunnel (float64, medians of 5 interleaved rounds on a CPU shared with other jobs, torch default 16 threads; rounds scattered ±50 %). Eager CPU steps therefore advect only the live slots (ParticleSystem3D.step(compact=True); finding them is a host sync, which costs nothing there and is never captured): 40.9 ms per step, ×1.47 the flow step instead of ×14.8. The arithmetic per particle is unchanged, so the live particles are the full ring's bit for bit (test); CUDA, eager or graphs, keeps the static path.

17. Fans, jets and vents (DESIGN.md 10.3)#

Actuator discs. A fan is a uniform force density over a thin disc (thickness thickness, default max(D/8, 2 h)), anti-aliased and normalised separately on the u-, v- and w-faces so the force adds up to exactly T n̂ (the normalisation runs over the whole disc before the domain clips it, so a disc centred on a symmetry plane puts exactly half of itself into a half-domain run, and the report doubles it back). Modes: "thrust" (value = T/(ρU²L²)), "pressure_jump" (T = Δp A) and "velocity": a PI controller in the step (no host sync) drives the measured disc-averaged axial velocity to value. The controller starts from the momentum-theory thrust 2A u_t (u_t − U n_x), with gains K_P = ½ g, K_I = g/τ, g = 2A max(|2u_t − U n_x|, |U n_x|, 0.1) (the derivative of the momentum-theory thrust with respect to the disc velocity) and τ = 4 D / max(|u_t|, |U n_x|, 0.1) (four disc transit times), and the thrust is clamped to ±(50 A (|u_t| + 1)² + |T_0|). swirl adds a solid-body tangential force whose torque matches the angular-momentum flux of a swirl of swirl × the disc velocity at the rim; its net force is removed per component (the samples of n̂ × r on a staggered grid only cancel when the centre is on the grid; before this, an off-grid fan pushed sideways: 1.9·10⁻³ of side momentum after 40 steps, now 10⁻¹⁷). Swirl about an axis cut by the symmetry plane is not mirror-symmetric and is ignored there with a note. fans_report(mean=…): thrust, pressure jump, disc and induced velocity, volume flow, ideal power T·u_d, target and the in-domain share; per-step series fan<i>_thrust, fan<i>_velocity, fan<i>_flow in physics_history().

Jets and vents. A jet is a mass source m = Q φ over a disc footprint (Q = speed·πD²/4) carrying the momentum m U_j n̂, its temperature m θ_j and, with dye_channel, smoke; a vent is the matching sink m = −Q φ, which removes the local velocity, temperature and smoke. The projection solves ∇²ψ = ∇·û − m, so the continuity residual is ∇·u − m (field("divergence")); a tunnel's outlet carries the inflow plus Σm h³. In a closed domain the sources must balance: an unbalanced set gets a note and the all-Neumann Poisson problem removes the excess uniformly (its compatibility condition — the zero mode is dropped), i.e. the residual becomes a uniform Q_net/V. A jet is a source rather than a penalised velocity region (DESIGN.md 10.3 suggested the latter) because the mass and momentum fluxes are then exact by construction, and a vent can remove the local fluid with whatever it carries.

The fluid the uniform removal takes out carries its temperature and smoke with it (review round 5 before the release): with Q_net > 0 in a closed or fully periodic box, PortSet.scalar_sources also subtracts b Δt (Qnet/V) c on every cell (PortSet.leak), and heat_balance books (Qnet/V) ∫θ dV as heat out of the ports. Before, the conservative update kept it — a source +c Qnet/V everywhere — so the mean temperature of a 32³ closed box fed by a θ = 1 jet (Q 0.0314) grew linearly at Q/V and would have passed the jet's temperature at t ≈ 32, while heat out of the ports read 0. Now the mean follows 1 − exp(−Q t / V): 0.4505 at t = 19.06 against 0.4504, max θ 0.936, residual 3 × 10⁻⁴ of the heat in. A net sink needs no term (the created fluid arrives at θ = 0 and dilutes).

Diagnostics (diagnostics=True; the model's record_diagnostics defaults to on): one device-side row per 10-step block (the block's last step, inside the CUDA graph, no host sync), copied to the host with the force history: t, dt, the CFL number of the step taken, Σ max|u_i|, the RMS and max continuity residual (∇·u − m), the relative Poisson residual of the last projection, and per field u, v, w, p, θ the change over the step (discrete L2, RMS, max, relative L2) and the heat-storage rate. diagnostics() adds step, steps_per_s (host-measured per advance call) and p_residual (the 2D name); diagnostics_csv(path) exports them.

Solver trace (trace(), trace_step(commit=False)): one step on the reference core with every Runge-Kutta stage captured as cell-centred fields — the stage value after advection, diffusion, fans and ports, after the penalisation and buoyancy, the projection's right-hand side, ψ, the correction −∇ψ, the projected velocity, θ — and norms (largest right-hand side, largest divergence after the projection, relative Poisson residual |Lψ − rhs|/|rhs|, largest correction). The run is untouched: the state is copied and every physics accumulator restored; commit=True then advances one normal step (it agrees with the traced one to the fused/reference round-off, not bit for bit). Measured on a heated sphere with a regulated fan (float64): projected divergence < 10⁻¹¹, Poisson residual < 10⁻¹⁰ in every stage.

PID time step (adaptive_dt = "pid"): the CFL rule jumps to the stability limit every step, so dt follows every flicker of max|u|. The controller acts on e = log(dt_target/dt_prev) with dt_target = pid_target (0.85) × the limit: dt = dt_prev exp(K_I e + K_P (e − e₁) + K_D (e − 2e₁ + e₂)), (K_I, K_P, K_D) = (0.3, 0.4, 0.05) (Söderlind-style digital control), the factor clamped to [0.5, 1.2] per step and dt never above the limit, so the CFL number never exceeds cfl. All state is on the device. Measured over 200 steps of an impulsively started sphere: step-to-step dt ratios within [0.5, 1.2], the CFL number never above 0.5 and settled at 0.85 × 0.5 within 10 % (the CFL rule sits at exactly 0.5).

Limitations — what to trust.

  • Heat transfer is validated for natural convection in an enclosure (Ra 10⁴–10⁵, 0.04 % from the spectral benchmark with a grid study), forced convection from a sphere (Re 50–100, inside the correlations' band; Re 50: +0.3 % from the Clift–Yuge centre at 24 cells/D; Re 100: +1.5 % from the Clift–Yuge centre at 24 cells/D) and the hydrostatic force on bodies. Mixed convection around bodies (Ri ~ 1 with a body) is computed by the same terms but has no 3D benchmark here.
  • The 3D u_max at Ra 10⁵ is 3.8 % below the two 3D references that report it and 1.9 % below a third (lattice Boltzmann) solution (§15.6, open; the 2D-limit velocities agree to 0.2 %).
  • Heated bodies need the momentum's resolution rules (≥ 16–24 cells across); the thermal boundary layer is thinner than the momentum one for Pr > 1, so high-Pr fluids (water, Pr ≈ 7) need ~√Pr times finer grids for the same accuracy — not validated here.
  • θ is not clipped: with sources, diffusion and jets it is a physical field, bounded only by the limiter's TVD property for advection.
  • Fans are uniformly loaded actuator discs (no blade-element loading, no tip loss); the momentum-theory comparison is laminar and confined (§17), and a heavily loaded disc that holds the flow back (C_T 0.8) shows a 3 % higher disc velocity than 1D theory (2 % of it unexplained).
  • Jets are volumetric sources of finite thickness (≥ 2 cells): a jet thinner than ~4 cells across is smeared. The laminar round jet is validated (§17); turbulent jets (whose spreading the LES models would have to produce) are not.
  • Particles are massless tracers (no inertia, no diffusion); a fixed ring per emitter caps the count (oldest recycled).
  • Not in this release (DESIGN.md 10.4): Lattice-Boltzmann solver, neural-operator surrogates.
